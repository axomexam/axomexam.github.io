    Indian Economy: An Introduction

    An economy is a system by which a society produces, distributes and consumes goods and services to satisfy human wants. In simple words, it answers three basic questions: what to produce, how to produce, and for whom to produce. Every economy must also decide how to use its scarce resources, and this choice is studied under the subject of economics.

    Economic systems are mainly of three types. In a capitalist or market economy, production is owned and controlled by private individuals and prices are decided by the forces of demand and supply with minimum government interference (for example the USA). In a socialist or command economy, the state owns the means of production, and a central authority decides what and how much to produce (for example the former USSR). India has chosen a mixed economy, in which both the public sector and the private sector work together. The public sector handles strategic and basic industries, while the private sector is free to work in most other areas.

    অৰ্থনীতি হৈছে এনে এটা ব্যৱস্থা যাৰ জৰিয়তে সমাজে মানুহৰ প্ৰয়োজন পূৰণ কৰিবলৈ সামগ্ৰী আৰু সেৱা উৎপাদন, বিতৰণ আৰু ভোগ কৰে। সৰলভাৱে ক'বলৈ গ'লে ই তিনিটা মূল প্ৰশ্নৰ উত্তৰ দিয়ে: কি উৎপাদন কৰিব, কেনেকৈ উৎপাদন কৰিব, আৰু কাৰ বাবে উৎপাদন কৰিব। প্ৰতিটো অৰ্থনীতিয়ে নিজৰ দুৰ্লভ সম্পদ কেনেকৈ ব্যৱহাৰ কৰিব সেই সিদ্ধান্তও ল'ব লাগে, আৰু এই নিৰ্বাচনকেই অৰ্থনীতি বিদ্যা বিষয়ত অধ্যয়ন কৰা হয়।

    National Income Accounting

    National income is the total money value of all final goods and services produced by a country's residents in a given financial year, plus net income earned from abroad. It is the most important measure of the economic health of a country. Only final goods and services are counted so that the same product is not counted twice; intermediate goods are excluded to avoid double counting.

    The most important concepts of national income are as follows. GDP or Gross Domestic Product is the total value of all final goods and services produced within the geographical boundary of a country in a year, irrespective of who produces them. GNP or Gross National Product is GDP plus net factor income from abroad (income earned by residents abroad minus income earned by foreigners in India). NNP or Net National Product is GNP minus depreciation. NNP at factor cost is called National Income.

    Money & Banking: RBI & Monetary Policy

    Money is anything which is generally accepted as a medium of exchange, a measure of value, a store of value and a standard of deferred payments. In the modern economy, money mainly exists as currency (notes and coins) issued by the government and the central bank, and as demand deposits kept in banks. In India, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) was established on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act of 1934, and it was nationalised in 1949. The RBI is the central bank of India and performs the role of banker to the government, banker to the banks, issuer of currency, controller of credit, and custodian of foreign exchange reserves.

    The RBI issues currency notes of all denominations except the one-rupee note, which is issued by the Government of India and signed by the Finance Secretary. The RBI acts as the lender of last resort for commercial banks, which means that when banks face a shortage of funds they can borrow from the RBI in times of difficulty. The headquarters of the RBI is in Mumbai.

    Commercial banks accept deposits and give loans.

    How to prepare Economics

    • Work through one topic at a time and read the explanation for every answer you miss.
    • Keep a one-page note of the facts you keep forgetting and revise it before each session.
    • Re-attempt the same topic after a few days to check whether it has stuck.
    • Practise in both Assamese and English so the same fact is recognised in either language.
    • Attempt a timed set of 20 questions once a topic is finished, to build exam speed.
