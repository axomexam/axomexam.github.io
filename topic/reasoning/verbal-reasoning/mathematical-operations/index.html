    Mathematical Operations: Types, Methods and Examples

    Mathematical operations is a reasoning topic in which the usual meanings of arithmetic signs are changed, or the positions of numbers and signs are interchanged, and the candidate has to evaluate the given expression by following the new rule. The question may state, for example, that plus means multiply and minus means divide, or that the given equation is wrong and two signs must be swapped to make it correct. The candidate must not use the normal meaning of the signs but must apply the substitution exactly as told.

    The special feature of this topic is that it combines simple arithmetic with careful observation. The calculation itself is easy; the difficulty is in following the changed meaning, respecting the order of operations and avoiding a careless slip. This article gives a complete deep-study revision of mathematical operations. It explains the basic terms, the types of questions, the BODMAS rule, the substitution method, the useful shortcuts, the common mistakes and a practical preparation plan so that the topic becomes a quick and reliable source of marks in APSC, ADRE, Assam Police, SSC and Railway examinations.

    Basic Terms

    • Operation: a mathematical action such as addition, subtraction, multiplication or division.
    • Symbol substitution: replacing the given signs with other signs according to a rule.
    • Interchange: swapping two signs or two numbers in the expression.
    • BODMAS: the order of operations, namely Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction.
    • Expression: a statement built from numbers and signs.
    • Equation: an expression with an equals sign, where both sides must balance.
    • Coded operation: a question in which a letter or symbol stands for an arithmetic operation.
    • Conditional operation: a question in which the operation to be used depends on a condition such as whether a number is even.
    • Balance: the state in which the left side of an equation equals the right side.
    • Verification: checking the answer by putting it back into the original rule.

    Core Concepts and Shortcuts

    • Always replace the signs first, and only then calculate; never calculate with the old meaning.
    • Apply BODMAS strictly after substitution, doing brackets first and subtraction last.
    • In sign interchange questions, change the sign exactly as said and then check both sides.
    • In number interchange questions, swap the numbers as well as the signs if asked, before calculating.
    • For coded operations, write the symbol against each operation in a small table before starting.
    • When the equation must be balanced, try each option in turn instead of guessing.
    • Keep the negative signs and brackets intact while substituting, because one lost sign changes the answer.

    Types of Questions

    The first type gives the new meaning of the signs and asks for the value of an expression. The second type gives an equation with one wrong sign and asks which two signs should be interchanged to make it correct. The third type replaces numbers with letters or letters with symbols, and the candidate must decode the expression. The fourth type is conditional, where the operation to be performed depends on the nature of the numbers. The fifth type presents an equation and asks which of the given alternatives is true when the substitution is applied. All these types use the same principle: convert the given expression into an ordinary one and then compute.

    Order of Operations and BODMAS

    BODMAS decides which operation is done first. Brackets are solved first, then orders or powers, then division, then multiplication, then addition and finally subtraction. This order is very important in mathematical operations because the changed signs can make a wrong order give a wrong answer. For example, if plus means multiply and multiply means plus, the candidate must not simply work from left to right but must decide which of the substituted operations has higher priority. A candidate who ignores BODMAS in such questions almost always selects the wrong option.

    Steps to Solve a Mathematical Operations Question

    First, read the rule of substitution carefully and write it down. Second, replace every sign in the expression with its new meaning, doing it one at a time and neatly. Third, apply BODMAS to the converted expression and calculate in small steps. Fourth, if the question is about balancing an equation, test each option until both sides match. Finally, verify the answer by re-reading the rule to ensure that no sign was left unchanged or changed twice.

The most common form is the sign-substitution expression, followed by the interchange form and the balancing form. Coded and conditional forms appear less often but are easy once the meaning is clear. Because the arithmetic is simple, the marks depend entirely on careful reading and correct order of operations.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Do not use the ordinary meaning of a sign after substitution.
    • Do not ignore BODMAS; always respect the order of operations.
    • Do not change a sign twice, once by mistake and once by rule.
    • In interchange questions, do not swap the signs without checking both sides afterwards.
    • Do not drop brackets or negative signs while converting the expression.

    কাৰণ চিহ্ন সলনি কৰাৰ পিছত ক্ৰিয়াৰ ক্ৰমে কোনটো গণনা আগতে কৰা হয় সেয়া নিৰ্ধাৰণ কৰে। প্ৰতিস্থাপন শুদ্ধ হ'লেও BODMAS উপেক্ষা কৰিলে ভুল উত্তৰ হ'ব পাৰে।

    চিহ্ন বিনিময়ৰ প্ৰশ্ন কেনেকৈ সমাধান কৰিম?

    এটাকৈ প্ৰতিটো বিকল্প পৰীক্ষা কৰা। প্ৰতিটো বিকল্পৰ বাবে কোৱাৰ দৰে দুটা চিহ্ন সলনি কৰি সমীকৰণৰ বাওঁ ফাল সোঁ ফালৰ সমান হয় নে নাই পৰীক্ষা কৰা।

    ক'ড কৰা ক্ৰিয়াৰ প্ৰশ্ন কি?

    ক'ড কৰা ক্ৰিয়াৰ প্ৰশ্নত এটা আখৰ বা চিহ্নই এটা গাণিতিক ক্ৰিয়াক সূচায়। আগতে প্ৰতিটো চিহ্নৰ অৰ্থ লিখা, তাৰ পিছত ৰাশিত সলনি কৰি গণনা কৰা।

    এনে প্ৰশ্নত আগতে কি কৰিব লাগে?

    সদায় আগতে দিয়া নিয়ম অনুসৰি চিহ্নবোৰ সলনি কৰা, তেতিয়াহে BODMAS অনুসৰণ কৰি গণনা কৰা। কেতিয়াও চিহ্নৰ সাধাৰণ অৰ্থৰে গণনা আৰম্ভ নকৰিবা।
