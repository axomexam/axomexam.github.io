      Sentence Rearrangement: Order, Cohesion and Solving Rules

      Sentence rearrangement asks a candidate to place a set of jumbled words, phrases or sentences in the correct order so that they form a meaningful whole. It tests the sense of logical order, the knowledge of grammar and the understanding of how sentences link through referring words and connectors.

      This deep-study note explains the terms, the rules that fix order, the question patterns, a step-by-step method, the traps and a preparation plan. After reading it, a candidate should be able to locate the opening sentence, arrange the middle with the help of connectors and pronouns, and identify the closing sentence.

      Basic Terms

      • Rearrangement: putting jumbled parts into a meaningful order.
      • Opening sentence: the sentence that introduces the topic and depends on nothing before it.
      • Closing sentence: the sentence that concludes or sums up the idea.
      • Connector: a word such as "however", "therefore" or "moreover" that links ideas.
      • Pronoun reference: a pronoun that points to a noun already mentioned, which fixes order.
      • Chronology: the order of time, which decides the sequence of events.
      • Cause and effect: an order in which the cause comes before the result.
      • General to specific: a common order where a general statement is followed by details.
      • Cohesion: the linking of sentences so that the passage reads as one unit.
      • Coherence: the logical flow of ideas in a passage.

      Core Concepts and Rules

      • The opening sentence is usually independent and introduces the topic; it does not begin with a pronoun or a connector.
      • A sentence that begins with "this", "he", "she", "they" or "it" cannot be the first, because the noun it refers to must come earlier.
      • Connectors indicate order: "first", "then", "next" and "finally" show sequence; "however" shows contrast; "therefore" shows result.
      • The cause comes before the effect, and the problem usually comes before the solution.
      • A definition or general statement comes before examples and details.
      • Sentences that share a subject or a theme tend to stay together.
      • A concluding sentence often contains words such as "thus", "in short" or "as a result".
      • Punctuation and articles give clues; "the" often follows a first mention with "a".
      • Time words such as "later", "soon" and "afterwards" fix the order of events.
      • Once an order is chosen, read the whole passage to check that it flows without breaks.

      Strategies for Rearrangement

      Start by finding the sentence that can stand first; it names the subject and needs no earlier reference. Then find the sentence that must come last, usually a conclusion. Next, pair the sentences that are linked by pronouns or connectors, such as a sentence with "this problem" following one that describes a problem. In a word-based question, look for the subject and verb first, then place the object, the modifiers and the adverbs in their natural positions. Finally, read the arranged passage and confirm that every connector and pronoun now has a clear reference.

      Types of Questions Asked

      The first pattern gives a paragraph of numbered sentences and asks for the correct order. The second gives jumbled words and asks for a meaningful sentence. The third gives a first or last sentence and asks for the order of the middle. The fourth asks which sentence should come at a given position. The fifth asks for the correct sequence of a set of phrases within a single sentence.

      Steps to Solve a Rearrangement Question

      First, read all the parts and grasp the general theme. Second, identify the opening sentence and the closing sentence. Third, find the links between sentences through pronouns, connectors and repeated words. Fourth, arrange the middle in logical order, checking cause and effect and chronology. Fifth, test the arrangement by reading it as a passage. Finally, confirm that no pronoun comes before its noun and no result comes before its cause.

      Examination Trends

      In Assam recruitment papers, paragraph rearrangement and jumbled-sentence questions appear regularly. Passages are usually short, factual or explanatory, and the links are made by clear connectors and pronouns. The tested skill is logical reading rather than deep grammar, so regular practice with short passages raises accuracy quickly.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Do not begin with a sentence that opens with a pronoun or a connector.
      • Do not place an effect before its cause.
      • Do not ignore chronological markers.
      • Do not break a pair of sentences that are linked by a pronoun.
      • Do not rely on one clue only; confirm with a second clue.
      • Do not forget to read the final order as a whole passage.

      How to Prepare This Topic

      Practise two rearrangement passages daily, and after each one write down the clue that fixed each link. Read the arranged passage aloud to hear whether it flows. Build a habit of scanning for pronouns and connectors first, because these carry the order.

      Also take a short news paragraph each day, cut it into sentences mentally and decide why each sentence comes where it does. This exercise trains the same skill from the other direction. Keep a list of the connectors that show contrast, result, addition and sequence, and revise it before tests.

      Quick Revision Tips

      • The first sentence needs no earlier reference.
      • Pronouns and connectors fix the order.
      • Cause before effect; general before specific.
      • The last sentence concludes the idea.
      • Read the final order as a passage to confirm.

      Final Revision Note

      On the last day, solve three passages and, for each, note the opening sentence, the closing sentence and the links between the rest. Confirm that no pronoun precedes its noun and no effect precedes its cause. Rearrangement rewards patient reading far more than speed, and a candidate who reads the final order once before answering seldom goes wrong.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What is sentence rearrangement?

      Sentence rearrangement means arranging jumbled words, phrases or sentences in the correct order so that they form a meaningful, coherent whole.

      How do I find the first sentence?

      The first sentence introduces the topic and needs no earlier reference. It usually contains no pronoun such as "he" or "this" and no connector such as "however" or "therefore".

      What role do connectors play?

      Connectors carry the logic of the passage. Words like "however", "therefore", "first" and "finally" tell you whether the next sentence contrasts, results, adds or continues, and so fix the order.

      Why must a pronoun not come before its noun?

      A pronoun stands for a noun that must already have appeared. If a sentence begins with "this problem", the problem must have been described earlier, so that sentence cannot come first.

      How can I improve accuracy quickly?

      Practise short passages daily, scan for pronouns and connectors first, and always read the final order as one passage to check that it flows before marking the answer.

      • আৰম্ভণিৰ বাক্য সাধাৰণতে স্বাধীন আৰু বিষয় আৰম্ভ কৰে; ই pronoun বা connector ৰে আৰম্ভ নহয়।
      • "this", "he", "she", "they", "it" ৰে আৰম্ভ হোৱা বাক্য প্ৰথম হ'ব নোৱাৰে, কাৰণ ইয়াৰ নাম আগতে থাকিব লাগে।
      • Connector এ ক্ৰম সূচায়: "first", "then", "next", "finally" ক্ৰম দেখুৱায়; "however" বিপৰীত; "therefore" ফলাফল।
      • কাৰণ ফলাফলৰ আগত আৰু সমস্যা সাধাৰণতে সমাধানৰ আগত আহে।
      • সংজ্ঞা বা সাধাৰণ বক্তব্য উদাহৰণ আৰু বিৱৰণৰ আগত আহে।
      • একে কৰ্তা বা বিষয় থকা বাক্যবোৰ একেলগে থাকে।
      • শেষ বাক্যত প্ৰায়ে "thus", "in short", "as a result" ৰ দৰে শব্দ থাকে।
      • যতিচিহ্ন আৰু article এ সংকেত দিয়ে; "a" ৰ প্ৰথম উল্লেখৰ পিছত প্ৰায়ে "the" আহে।
      • "later", "soon", "afterwards" ৰ দৰে সময়বোধক শব্দই ঘটনাৰ ক্ৰম নিৰ্ণয় কৰে।
      • ক্ৰম বাছনি কৰাৰ পিছত গোটেই অনুচ্ছেদ পঢ়ি ভাঙন নোহোৱাকৈ বৈ যায় নে নাই নিশ্চিত কৰক।

      প্ৰথমে কোনটো বাক্য আগত থিয় হ'ব পাৰে তাক বিচাৰক; ই বিষয়ৰ নাম দিয়ে আৰু আগৰ উল্লেখ নালাগে। তাৰ পিছত শেষত অহা বাক্য, সাধাৰণতে সিদ্ধান্তটো, বিচাৰক। তাৰ পিছত pronoun বা connector ৰে যুক্ত বাক্যবোৰ যোৰাওক, যেনে সমস্যা বৰ্ণনা কৰা বাক্যৰ পিছত "this problem" থকা বাক্য। শব্দভিত্তিক প্ৰশ্নত প্ৰথমে কৰ্তা আৰু ক্ৰিয়া, তাৰ পিছত কৰ্ম, modifier আৰু ক্ৰিয়া বিশেষণ স্বাভাৱিক স্থানত বহুৱাওক। শেষত সজোৱা অনুচ্ছেদ পঢ়ি প্ৰতিটো connector আৰু pronoun ৰ স্পষ্ট উল্লেখ আছে নে নাই নিশ্চিত কৰক।

      সোধা প্ৰশ্নৰ প্ৰকাৰ

      সঘনাই সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

      Sentence rearrangement কি?

      Sentence rearrangement মানে এলমেলি দিয়া শব্দ, পদ বা বাক্যবোৰ সঠিক ক্ৰমত সজাই অৰ্থপূৰ্ণ, সংহত গোট কৰা।

      প্ৰথম বাক্য কেনেকৈ বিচাৰিম?

      প্ৰথম বাক্যই বিষয় আৰম্ভ কৰে আৰু আগৰ উল্লেখ নালাগে। ই সাধাৰণতে "he" বা "this" ৰ দৰে pronoun আৰু "however" বা "therefore" ৰ দৰে connector ৰে আৰম্ভ নহয়।

      Connector ৰ ভূমিকা কি?

      Connector এ অনুচ্ছেদৰ যুক্তি বহন কৰে। "however", "therefore", "first", "finally" ৰ দৰে শব্দই পিছৰ বাক্যই বিপৰীত, ফলাফল, সংযোজন নে ক্ৰম CONTINUE কৰিছে কয় আৰু ক্ৰম নিৰ্ণয় কৰে।

      Pronoun নামৰ আগত কিয় থাকিব নোৱাৰে?

      Pronoun এ এনে নামৰ ঠাই লয় যি আগতে আহি থাকিব লাগে। বাক্য "this problem" ৰে আৰম্ভ হ'লে সমস্যাটো আগতে বৰ্ণনা কৰা থাকিব লাগে, গতিকে সেই বাক্য প্ৰথম হ'ব নোৱাৰে।

      শুদ্ধতা দ্ৰুত কেনেকৈ বঢ়াম?

      দৈনিক চুটি অনুচ্ছেদ অভ্যাস কৰক, প্ৰথমে pronoun আৰু connector বিচাৰক, আৰু উত্তৰ চিহ্নিত কৰাৰ আগতে শেষ ক্ৰমটো এটা অনুচ্ছেদ হিচাপে পঢ়ি বৈ যায় নে নাই পৰীক্ষা কৰক।
