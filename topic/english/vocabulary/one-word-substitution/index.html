    Q1. Choose the correct one word for the given phrase: "a person who cannot read or write".
    দিয়া পদটোৰ বাবে সঠিক একক শব্দটো বাছি উলিয়াওক: "যিয়ে পঢ়িব নাইবা লিখিব নোৱাৰে"।
    (A) Illiterate Illiterate
    (B) Ignorant Ignorant
    (C) Uneducated Uneducated
    (D) Immature Immature
    Answer: 'Illiterate' is the correct one-word substitute, meaning a person who cannot read or write.
    সঠিক উত্তৰ হৈছে 'Illiterate'। ইয়াৰ অৰ্থ হৈছে: যিয়ে পঢ়িব নাইবা লিখিব নোৱাৰে।
    Explanation: 'Illiterate' is formed from the Latin prefix 'il-' (not) and 'litteratus' (lettered), so it literally means 'not lettered'. It is the exact term for a person who cannot read or write, while 'ignorant' means lacking knowledge in general.
    'Illiterate' শব্দটো লেটিন 'il-' (নহয়) আৰু 'litteratus' (আখৰজ্ঞ)ৰ পৰা গঠিত, অৰ্থাৎ 'আখৰ নজনা'। যিয়ে পঢ়িব বা লিখিব নোৱাৰে তাৰ বাবেই এই শব্দ প্ৰযোজ্য; 'ignorant' ৰ অৰ্থ সাধাৰণ জ্ঞানহীন।

    One Word Substitution: Meaning, Formation and Selection Rules

    One word substitution is the use of a single, precise word in place of a longer phrase or clause. Instead of saying "a person who does not believe in the existence of God", one says "atheist". The topic tests concise expression, and it rewards candidates who know exact words rather than approximate ones. It appears in the English paper of almost every competitive examination.

    This deep-study note explains the basic terms, the rules of word formation, the families into which substitutions fall, the question patterns, a step-by-step method, the common traps and a preparation plan.     Basic Terms

    • One word substitution: replacing a phrase or clause with one exact word.
    • Person noun: a word for a kind of person, as "philanthropist" for one who loves mankind.
    • Place noun: a word for a place, as "aviary" for a place where birds are kept.
    • Practice or study noun: a word for a study or practice, as "ornithology" for the study of birds.
    • Quality noun: a word for a quality, as "omnipotence" for unlimited power.
    • Root: the base that carries the core meaning, as "cide" (killing) in "homicide".
    • Prefix: an addition at the start, as "mis-" (wrongly) in "misanthrope".
    • Suffix: an addition at the end, as "-logy" (study of) in "biology".
    • Latin and Greek roots: the source of most technical substitutions, as "aqua" (water) and "bios" (life).
    • Exactness: the single word must carry the full meaning of the phrase, no more and no less.

    Core Concepts and Rules

    • The substituted word must match the part of speech required by the sentence.
    • Gender and number matter; some words end in "-ess" for the feminine, as "hostess".
    • Roots decide meaning: "anthrop" (man), "biblio" (book), "chrono" (time), "geo" (earth).
    • Suffixes such as "-ology" (study), "-phobia" (fear), "-cide" (killing) and "-graphy" (writing) recur constantly.
    • Some words denote a person, some a place, some a thing and some an action; the question must be read for the type required.
    • Similar-sounding words must not be confused, as "amiable" (friendly) and "amiable"/"amicable" (peaceful).
    • A word may exist in several forms; the sentence decides whether a noun, a verb or an adjective is needed.
    • One-word substitutions are often built from the same roots, so learning roots gives disproportionate returns.
    • Do not accept a two-word answer when the question demands one word.
    • The correct option is the one whose whole meaning equals the phrase, not merely overlaps it.

    Families of One Word Substitutions

    Substitutions gather into recognisable families. Words for persons include "misogynist" (one who hates women) and "connoisseur" (an expert judge). Words for places include "apiary" (bees), "granary" (grain) and "armoury" (weapons). Words for studies include "etymology" (origin of words) and "astronomy" (stars). Words for government and rule include "democracy", "autocracy" and "bureaucracy". Words for qualities include "omniscient" (all-knowing) and "ubiquitous" (present everywhere). Learning by family is far more efficient than learning in alphabetical order.

    Types of Questions Asked

    The first pattern gives a phrase and asks for the one word. The second gives the word and asks for its meaning. The third places a blank in a sentence and asks for the exact word. The fourth asks a candidate to choose the word that best replaces an underlined expression. The fifth tests roots by asking which word means a given idea. Direct phrase-to-word questions dominate the section.

    Steps to Solve a One Word Substitution Question

    First, read the phrase and decide what kind of word is needed: a person, a place, a quality, a study or an action. Second, look for a root in the phrase, such as "man" in "mankind" or "book" in "book-lover". Third, recall the suffix that usually goes with the family, such as "-phile" for a lover and "-phobia" for a fear. Fourth, eliminate options that give the wrong part of speech. Fifth, check that the meaning is exact and not approximate. Finally, substitute the chosen word into the sentence and confirm that it reads correctly.

    Examination Trends

    In Assam recruitment papers, substitutions for persons and studies are the most frequent, followed by places and qualities. Words built on Greek and Latin roots, such as "bibliophile", "xenophobia" and "misanthrope", recur. Because the vocabulary is finite and repeated, a candidate who learns the standard list of a few hundred words can answer the whole section with confidence.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Do not give a two-word answer when one word is required.
    • Do not confuse similar roots, as "philanthropist" (loves mankind) and "philatelist" (collects stamps).
    • Do not ignore the part of speech required by the sentence.
    • Do not choose a near word; the substitute must be exact.
    • Do not mix up place words, as "apiary" (bees) and "aviary" (birds).
    • Do not forget feminine forms when the phrase names a female.

    How to Prepare This Topic

    Build four lists: persons, places, studies and qualities, each word written with its meaning and root. Learn the common roots and suffixes first, because they let you decode new words. Revise one family a day and test yourself by giving the word for a meaning and then the meaning for a word.

    Read the lists aloud, since sound helps memory, and group words that share a root, such as all "-phobia" words together. Keep a running list of the words you miss and revise them before every test. Because one-word substitution also helps in comprehension, this preparation improves the whole English paper.

    Quick Revision Tips

    • Decide first whether the answer is a person, place, study or quality.
    • Roots and suffixes decode most words.
    • Learn words in families, not alphabetically.
    • The substitute must be exact, not approximate.
    • Check the part of speech before answering.

    Final Revision Note

    On the last day, revise the root and suffix list, then run through your family lists quickly. Take twenty mixed phrases and supply the word for each without looking. Re-read the ones you missed and write each with its meaning. When the root, the suffix, the part of speech and the meaning all agree, the substitution is correct.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is one word substitution?

    One word substitution means replacing a longer phrase or clause with a single exact word, such as using "atheist" for a person who does not believe in God.

    How can I remember so many words?

    Learn the common roots and suffixes first, and group words into families of persons, places, studies and qualities. This lets you decode and recall words far faster than memorising a plain list.

    What is the difference between an apiary and an aviary?

    An apiary is a place where bees are kept, while an aviary is a place where birds are kept. Such place words are often paired in exam options to test exact knowledge.

    Do all substitutions end in a suffix?

    Most do, such as "-logy" for a study, "-phobia" for a fear and "-cide" for killing, but some are simple words. The suffix is a strong clue, not a rule.

    Which substitutions are most important for Assam exams?

    Words for persons and studies built on Greek and Latin roots, such as bibliophile, xenophobia, misanthrope and philanthropy, are the most frequently asked and should be learnt first.

    One Word Substitution প্ৰশ্ন সমাধানৰ স্তৰ

    প্ৰথমে পদটো পঢ়ি কি ধৰণৰ শব্দ লাগে — ব্যক্তি, স্থান, গুণ, বিদ্যা নে কাৰ্য — নিৰ্ণয় কৰক। দ্বিতীয়ে পদত root বিচাৰক, যেনে "mankind" ত "man" বা "book-lover" ত "book"। তৃতীয়ে পৰিয়ালৰ সৈতে যোৱা suffix মনত পেলাওক, যেনে প্ৰেমিকৰ বাবে "-phile" আৰু ভয়ৰ বাবে "-phobia"। চতুৰ্থে ভুল part of speech দিয়া বিকল্প আতৰাওক। পঞ্চমে অৰ্থ সঠিক, আনুমানিক নহয় নিশ্চিত কৰক। শেষত বাছনি কৰা শব্দ বাক্যত বহুৱাই পঢ়ক।

    • উত্তৰ দিয়াৰ আগতে part of speech চাওক।

    শেষ পুনৰালোচনা

    শেষ দিনা root আৰু suffix তালিকা পুনৰালোচনা কৰি পৰিয়ালৰ তালিকাবোৰ দ্ৰুতভাৱে চাওক। কুৰিটা মিশ্ৰিত পদ লৈ নোচোৱাকৈ প্ৰতিটোৰ শব্দ দিয়ক। বাদ পৰাবোৰ পুনৰ পঢ়ি অৰ্থসহ লিখক। Root, suffix, part of speech আৰু অৰ্থ চাৰিও মিলিলে substitution শুদ্ধ।

    Root আৰু Suffix বিভাজনৰ অভ্যাস

    অচিনাকি substitution এটাক ভাঙি চাব লাগে — আগে মূল (root), তাৰ পিছত উপসৰ্গ বা অনুসৰ্গ। "misogynist" ৰ "mis-" মানে বিদ্বেষ আৰু "gyn" মানে নাৰী, গতিকে শব্দটোৱে নাৰী-বিদ্বেষী ব্যক্তিক বুজায়। "misanthrope" ৰ "misan-" মানে মানুহৰ বিদ্বেষ, গতিকে ই মানৱ-বিদ্বেষী। এইদৰে "philanthropist" ৰ "phil-" মানে প্ৰেম আৰু "anthrop" মানে মানুহ, গতিকে ই মানৱপ্ৰেমী। Greek আৰু Latin উপসৰ্গৰ সৰু তালিকা এটা মনত ৰাখিলে — "bene-" (ভাল), "mal-" (বেয়া), "anti-" (বিৰোধী), "auto-" (নিজে), "cide" (বধ), "logy" (অধ্যয়ন) — এশৰো অধিক word substitution অনুমান নকৰি ভাঙি বুজিব পৰা যায়। দিনে দহটা অচিনাকি শব্দ এইদৰে ভাঙি নিজে অৰ্থ লিখাৰ অভ্যাস কৰক; কেইসপ্তাহতে শব্দ-ভাঙি-পঢ়াৰ অভ্যাস এটা শক্তিশালী কৌশল হৈ পৰিব। এই কৌশলৰ ফলত নতুন শব্দ লগা মাত্ৰেই তাৰ ভিতৰৰ মূল চিনি অৰ্থ অনুমান কৰিব পৰা যায়, আৰু পৰীক্ষাত সময়ো সাঁচে।

    সঘনাই সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

    One word substitution কি?

    One word substitution মানে দীঘল পদ বা clause ৰ ঠাইত এটা সঠিক শব্দ ব্যৱহাৰ কৰা, যেনে ঈশ্বৰত অবিশ্বাসী ব্যক্তিৰ বাবে "atheist"।

    ইমান শব্দ কেনেকৈ মনত ৰাখিম?

    আগতে সাধাৰণ root আৰু suffix শিকি ব্যক্তি, স্থান, বিদ্যা, গুণ পৰিয়ালত শব্দ গোটাওক। ইয়াৰ দ্বাৰা শব্দ ভাঙি মনত ৰখাটো সাধাৰণ তালিকাতকৈ বহু দ্ৰুত হয়।

    Apiary আৰু aviary ৰ পাৰ্থক্য কি?

    Apiary হৈছে মৌমাখি ৰখা ঠাই, আনহাতে aviary হৈছে চৰাই ৰখা ঠাই। এনে স্থানৰ শব্দ প্ৰায়ে সঠিক জ্ঞান পৰীক্ষা কৰিবলৈ যোৰ পাতি দিয়া হয়।

    সকলো substitution suffix ৰে শেষ হয়নে?

    বেছিভাগ হয়, যেনে বিদ্যাৰ বাবে "-logy", ভয়ৰ বাবে "-phobia", বধৰ বাবে "-cide", কিন্তু কিছু সাধাৰণ শব্দ। Suffix শক্তিশালী সংকেত, নিয়ম নহয়।

    অসমৰ পৰীক্ষাৰ বাবে কোন substitution বেছি গুৰুত্বপূৰ্ণ?

    Greek-Latin root ৰ ব্যক্তি আৰু বিদ্যাৰ শব্দ, যেনে bibliophile, xenophobia, misanthrope, philanthropy আটাইতকৈ বেছি সোধা হয় আৰু আগতে শিকা উচিত।
