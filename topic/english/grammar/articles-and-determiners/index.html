      Articles and Determiners: Types, Rules and Examples

      Articles and determiners are the small words that stand before a noun and tell us whether the noun is specific or general, counted or uncounted, near or far. The articles are a, an and the; determiners include this, that, these, those, my, your, some, any, much, many, few, little, each, every, either, neither, all, both and the numbers. They look minor, but they decide a large number of marks in the English section because a single missing or wrong article can change the meaning of a sentence completely.

      Articles and determiners are asked through fill in the blanks, error spotting, sentence correction and cloze tests in APSC, ADRE, Assam Police, SSC and Railway examinations. The topic is also closely tied to nouns and to subject-verb agreement, because a determiner fixes the number of the noun that follows. This deep-study note gives the basic terms, the core rules, the kinds of questions, the step-by-step method, the common mistakes and a practical preparation plan.

      Basic Terms

      • Definite article: "the", used for a particular noun already known to the reader or listener.
      • Indefinite article: "a" and "an", used for one non-specific item of a singular countable noun.
      • Demonstrative: this, that, these and those, which point to a noun and show nearness or distance.
      • Possessive determiner: my, your, his, her, its, our and their, which show ownership.
      • Quantifier: some, any, much, many, few, little, a lot of, which show quantity.
      • Distributive: each, every, either and neither, which refer to members of a group one by one.
      • Countable noun: a noun that can be counted and has a plural, such as "book" and "books".
      • Uncountable noun: a noun that cannot be counted directly, such as water, money and information.
      • Zero article: the absence of an article, as before most proper nouns and plural general nouns.
      • Determiner order: the sequence in which determiners are placed before a noun.

      Core Concepts and Rules

      • "A" is used before a word beginning with a consonant sound and "an" before a word beginning with a vowel sound, decided by sound and not by spelling.
      • So we say "a university", "a one-rupee coin" and "an hour", "an honest man" and "an MLA".
      • "The" is used for something already mentioned, for something unique, before superlatives, before the names of rivers, seas, groups of islands and holy books, and before a singular noun that stands for a whole class.
      • "The" is also used before the names of newspapers, famous buildings and the plural names of a country's people, as in "the Assamese".
      • No article is used before proper nouns, before abstract nouns used in a general sense, before the names of languages, meals, games and most diseases, and before uncountable nouns used generally.
      • Some is used in affirmative sentences and any in negatives and questions, though "any" is used in offers and requests, as in "Would you like any tea?".
      • Much goes with uncountable nouns and many with countable nouns; a little goes with uncountable and a few with countable nouns.
      • "A few" and "a little" mean some but not much, while "few" and "little" mean hardly any, and the article changes the meaning.
      • Each is used for two or more taken separately, every for three or more taken together, and either and neither for two only.
      • Only one central determiner can precede a noun, so we cannot say "the my book"; we say "my book" or "that book of mine".
      • With "of", the determiner becomes a pronoun: "some of the students", "each of them", "none of the money".

      Types of Articles and Determiners Questions

      The first type gives a sentence with a blank and four options, of which three are articles or determiners. The second type underlines an article or determiner and asks for the correction. The third type tests the difference between "a few" and "few", "a little" and "little", "some" and "any". The fourth type tests articles with unique things, superlatives and proper names. The fifth type tests the determiner that agrees with a countable or uncountable noun, and the sixth type is a cloze passage where a short text must be filled with the right determiners.

      Steps to Solve an Articles and Determiners Question

      First, find the noun that follows the blank and decide whether it is countable or uncountable, singular or plural. Second, decide whether the noun is specific or general, because specific nouns take "the" and general singular countable nouns take "a" or "an". Third, if the article is a or an, say the next word aloud and listen to its first sound. Fourth, if the blank is a quantifier, match it to the noun - much and little with uncountable, many and few with countable. Fifth, check that only one central determiner is present. Finally, read the whole sentence to confirm that the meaning is natural.

      Examination Trends

      Articles and determiners appear in almost every English paper, usually as part of error spotting and cloze tests. The favourite traps are "an" before a vowel letter that makes a consonant sound, as in "a European", and the missing article before a superlative. Questions on "few" versus "a few" and "little" versus "a little" are repeated because the article changes the meaning. Determiners with uncountable nouns, such as "much information" and "a piece of advice", are also common, and a candidate who reads the noun first usually avoids these traps.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Do not choose "an" by the letter a word starts with; choose it by the first sound.
      • Do not put "a" or "an" before an uncountable noun; say "information", not "an information".
      • Do not confuse "few" (hardly any) with "a few" (some) or "little" with "a little".
      • Do not use "many" with an uncountable noun or "much" with a countable noun.
      • Do not use two central determiners together, such as "the my friend".
      • Do not use "the" before a general plural noun, as in "The books are useful" when you mean books in general.

      How to Prepare This Topic

      Start by making three lists: nouns that take a or an, nouns that take much or many, and nouns that take few or little. Read the lists aloud so that the sounds and the quantities become automatic. Then take short paragraphs from a newspaper and mark every article and determiner, noting why each is used or why it is absent, because the zero article is tested as often as the articles themselves.

      A useful habit is to keep a "sound list" of words that begin with a vowel letter but a consonant sound, such as university, European and one, and words that begin with a silent letter, such as hour, honest and honour. Revise this list before every test, because these pairs are the examiner's favourite traps. Twenty questions a day for a fortnight, mixing articles with quantifiers, makes this topic quick and dependable and also strengthens subject-verb agreement and error spotting.

      Quick Revision Tips

      • A and an are decided by sound, not by spelling.
      • The is used for specific, unique and superlative nouns.
      • No article before general abstract and plural nouns, proper names, meals, games and languages.
      • Much and little go with uncountable nouns; many and few go with countable nouns.
      • "A few" and "a little" mean some; "few" and "little" mean hardly any.
      • Only one central determiner before a noun.

      Final Revision Note

      In the final revision, take ten sentences and fill in the correct article or determiner, saying each following word aloud to test the sound. Then correct one quantifier mistake in each sentence. Note down the traps you fall into and redrill only those. Because the noun decides the word that stands before it, reading the noun first is the master habit, and with daily practice articles and determiners becomes a quick and reliable source of marks in the English paper.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What is the difference between an article and a determiner?

      Articles are a, an and the, while determiners are a wider group that includes articles as well as this, some, each, my and many, all of which come before a noun and limit it.

      When do we use "a" and when "an"?

      "A" is used before a consonant sound and "an" before a vowel sound, decided by sound and not spelling, as in "a university" and "an hour".

      What is the difference between "few" and "a few"?

      "Few" means hardly any and has a negative sense, while "a few" means some and has a positive sense; the article changes the meaning.

      When is no article used before a noun?

      No article is used before proper nouns, general plural nouns, abstract nouns used generally, and the names of meals, games, languages and most diseases.

      Why are articles and determiners important for competitive exams?

      They are tested in fill in the blanks, error spotting and cloze tests, and they also decide subject-verb agreement, so they carry marks in every English paper.

      • "of" ৰ লগত determiner এটা pronoun হৈ পৰে: "some of the students", "each of them", "none of the money"।

      প্ৰশ্নৰ প্ৰকাৰ

      প্ৰথম প্ৰকাৰত খালী ঠাই থকা এটা বাক্য আৰু চাৰিটা বিকল্প দিয়া হয়, য'ত তিনিটা article বা determiner। দ্বিতীয় প্ৰকাৰত article বা determiner দাগি দি শুধৰণি বিচৰা হয়। তৃতীয় প্ৰকাৰত "a few" আৰু "few", "a little" আৰু "little", "some" আৰু "any" ৰ পাৰ্থক্য পৰীক্ষা কৰা হয়। চতুৰ্থ প্ৰকাৰত অদ্বিতীয় বস্তু, superlative আৰু proper name ৰ লগত article পৰীক্ষা কৰা হয়। পঞ্চম প্ৰকাৰত countable নে uncountable noun ৰ লগত মিলা determiner পৰীক্ষা কৰা হয়, আৰু ষষ্ঠ প্ৰকাৰত এটা চুটি texto খালী ঠাই শুদ্ধ determiner ৰে পূৰাবলগীয়া cloze passage দিয়া হয়।

      সমাধানৰ স্তৰ

      প্ৰথমে খালী ঠাইৰ পিছত অহা noun টো বিচাৰি সেয়া countable নে uncountable, একবচন নে বহুবচন নিৰ্ণয় কৰক। দ্বিতীয়ে, noun টো নিৰ্দিষ্ট নে সাধাৰণ নিৰ্ণয় কৰক, কাৰণ নিৰ্দিষ্ট noun-এ "the" আৰু সাধাৰণ একবচন countable noun-এ "a" বা "an" লয়। তৃতীয়ে, article a নে an হ'লে পিছৰ শব্দটো উচ্চস্বৰে কৈ প্ৰথম ধ্বনি শুনক। চতুৰ্থে, খালী ঠাই quantifier হ'লে noun-ৰ লগত মিলাওক - much, little uncountable ৰ লগত আৰু many, few countable ৰ লগত। পঞ্চমে, মাত্ৰ এটা central determiner আছে নে পৰীক্ষা কৰক। শেষত, গোটেই বাক্য পঢ়ি অৰ্থ স্বাভাৱিক নে নিশ্চিত হওক।

      পৰীক্ষাৰ প্ৰৱণতা

      Article আৰু determiner প্ৰায় প্ৰতিটো ইংৰাজী পত্ৰতে আহে, সাধাৰণতে error spotting আৰু cloze test ৰ অংশ হিচাপে। প্ৰিয় ফান্দবোৰ হৈছে - স্বৰবৰ্ণৰ আখৰেৰে আৰম্ভ হ'লেও ব্যঞ্জনধ্বনি থকা শব্দৰ আগত "an" দিয়া, যেনে "a European", আৰু superlative ৰ আগত article এৰি দিয়া। "few" বনাম "a few" আৰু "little" বনাম "a little" ৰ প্ৰশ্ন বাৰে বাৰে আহে, কাৰণ article-এ অৰ্থ সলায়। "much information", "a piece of advice" আদি uncountable noun-ৰ লগত determiner-ও সঘন, আৰু যিজনে প্ৰথমে noun পঢ়ে তেওঁ সাধাৰণতে এই ফান্দবোৰ এৰাই যায়।

      সাধাৰণ ভুল

      • শব্দৰ প্ৰথম আখৰ চাই "an" নুবাচিব; প্ৰথম ধ্বনি চাই বাছক।
      • Uncountable noun-ৰ আগত "a" বা "an" নিদিব; "information" লিখক, "an information" নহয়।
      • "few" (প্ৰায় নাই) আৰু "a few" (অলপ), বা "little" আৰু "a little" মিলাই নিদিব।
      • Uncountable noun-ৰ লগত "many", বা countable noun-ৰ লগত "much" নিদিব।
      • দুটা central determiner একেলগে নিদিব, যেনে "the my friend"।
      • সাধাৰণ বহুবচন noun-ৰ আগত "the" নিদিব, যেনে "The books are useful" যেতিয়া সাধাৰণ অৰ্থত books কয়।

      প্ৰস্তুতি কেনেকৈ কৰিব

      আৰম্ভণিতে তিনি তালিকা বনাওক: a বা an লোৱা noun, much বা many লোৱা noun, আৰু few বা little লোৱা noun। তালিকাবোৰ উচ্চস্বৰে পঢ়ক যাতে ধ্বনি আৰু পৰিমাণ স্বাভাৱিক হৈ পৰে। তাৰ পিছত বাতৰি কাকতৰ চুটি অনুচ্ছেদ লৈ প্ৰতিটো article আৰু determiner চিহ্নিত কৰি কিয় ব্যৱহৃত হৈছে বা কিয় নাই টুকি লওক, কাৰণ zero article-ও article-ৰ দৰেই সোধা হয়।

      এটা উপযোগী অভ্যাস হ'ল - স্বৰবৰ্ণৰ আখৰেৰে আৰম্ভ হৈ ব্যঞ্জনধ্বনি থকা শব্দৰ এটা "ধ্বনি তালিকা" ৰাখক, যেনে university, European, one, আৰু silent আখৰেৰে আৰম্ভ হোৱা শব্দ, যেনে hour, honest, honour। প্ৰতিটো পৰীক্ষাৰ আগে এই তালিকা পুনৰাবৃত্তি কৰক, কাৰণ এই যোৰবোৰ পৰীক্ষকৰ প্ৰিয় ফান্দ। দৈনিক বিশটা প্ৰশ্ন, article আৰু quantifier মিহলাই, দুসপ্তাহ অভ্যাস কৰিলে এই বিষয় দ্ৰুত আৰু নিৰ্ভৰযোগ্য হৈ পৰে, লগতে subject-verb agreement আৰু error spotting-ও সবল হয়।

      দ্ৰুত পুনৰালোচনাৰ টিপছ

      • a আৰু an ধ্বনিৰ দ্বাৰা নিৰ্ণয় হয়, বানানৰ দ্বাৰা নহয়।
      • the নিৰ্দিষ্ট, অদ্বিতীয় আৰু superlative noun ৰ বাবে।
      • সাধাৰণ abstract আৰু বহুবচন noun, proper name, ভোজন, খেল আৰু ভাষাৰ আগত article নাই।
      • Much আৰু little uncountable ৰ লগত; many আৰু few countable ৰ লগত।
      • "A few" আৰু "a little" মানে অলপ; "few" আৰু "little" মানে প্ৰায় নাই।
      • noun-ৰ আগত মাত্ৰ এটা central determiner।

      শেষ পুনৰালোচনা

      শেষ পুনৰালোচনাত দহটা বাক্য লৈ শুদ্ধ article বা determiner ভৰাওক, প্ৰতিটো পিছৰ শব্দ উচ্চস্বৰে কৈ ধ্বনি পৰীক্ষা কৰক। তাৰ পিছত প্ৰতিটো বাক্যত এটা quantifier ভুল শুধৰাওক। যিবোৰ ফান্দত পৰে সেইবোৰ টুকি লৈ কেৱল সেইবোৰ অভ্যাস কৰক। noun-এ তাৰ আগত অহা শব্দ নিৰ্ণয় কৰে বাবে প্ৰথমে noun পঢ়াটোৱে মুখ্য অভ্যাস, আৰু নিত্য অভ্যাসত article আৰু determiner ইংৰাজী পত্ৰত দ্ৰুত আৰু নিৰ্ভৰযোগ্য নম্বৰৰ উৎস হৈ পৰে।

      সঘনাই সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

      Article আৰু determiner ৰ পাৰ্থক্য কি?

      Article হৈছে a, an, the; আনহাতে determiner এটা ডাঙৰ গোট য'ত article ৰ লগতে this, some, each, my, many আদি থাকে, আৰু এইবোৰ noun-ৰ আগত আহি তাৰ পৰিসৰ নিৰ্ধাৰণ কৰে।

      "a" আৰু "an" কেতিয়া ব্যৱহাৰ কৰা হয়?

      ব্যঞ্জনধ্বনিৰ আগত "a" আৰু স্বৰধ্বনিৰ আগত "an", ধ্বনিৰ ভিত্তিত বানানৰ নহয়, যেনে "a university" আৰু "an hour"।

      "few" আৰু "a few" ৰ পাৰ্থক্য কি?

      "few" মানে প্ৰায় নাই আৰু নঞৰ্থক ভাৱ, আনহাতে "a few" মানে অলপ আৰু ধনাত্মক ভাৱ; article-এ অৰ্থ সলনি কৰে।

      কেতিয়া noun-ৰ আগত article নালাগে?

      Proper noun, সাধাৰণ বহুবচন noun, সাধাৰণ অৰ্থত abstract noun, আৰু ভোজন, খেল, ভাষা আৰু বেছিভাগ ৰোগৰ নামৰ আগত article নালাগে।

      প্ৰতিযোগিতামূলক পৰীক্ষাৰ বাবে article আৰু determiner কিয় গুৰুত্বপূৰ্ণ?

      ইহঁত fill in the blanks, error spotting আৰু cloze test ত সোধা হয়, লগতে subject-verb agreement-ও নিৰ্ণয় কৰে, সেয়ে প্ৰতিটো ইংৰাজী পত্ৰতে নম্বৰ বহন কৰে।
