      Direct and Indirect Speech (Narration)

      Conversion of direct speech into indirect speech with rules for tense, pronouns, questions, imperatives and exclamations asked in Assam competitive exams.

      (A) He said that he was tired.   (B) He said that I am tired.   (C) He says that he is tired.   (D) He said that he is tired.
      Answer: (A) He said that he was tired. — In indirect speech the present 'am' changes to the past 'was' and 'I' changes to 'he'.
      He said that he was tired.

      Direct and Indirect Speech (Narration): Types, Rules and Examples

      Narration is the way of reporting the words of a speaker. When we repeat the exact words of the speaker inside inverted commas, it is called direct speech, as in He said, "I am busy". When we report the meaning of those words in our own words, without inverted commas, it is called indirect speech, as in He said that he was busy. The change from direct to indirect speech is not a mere copying of words; the reporting verb, the pronouns, the tense and the words showing time and place all change in a fixed pattern.

      Direct and indirect speech is a standard topic in APSC, ADRE, Assam Police, SSC and Railway examinations, and it is tested through direct change of narration, error spotting and sentence improvement. It draws directly on tense, pronouns and subject-verb agreement, so a small mistake in any one of them spoils the whole answer. This deep-study note gives the basic terms, the core rules, the kinds of questions, the step-by-step method, the common mistakes and a practical preparation plan so that a candidate can convert any sentence quickly and correctly.

      Basic Terms

      • Direct speech: the exact words of the speaker placed inside inverted commas, as in She said, "I am happy".
      • Indirect speech: the same words reported without inverted commas, as in She said that she was happy.
      • Reporting verb: the verb that introduces the speech, such as said, told, asked and requested.
      • Reported speech: the actual words of the speaker that are being reported.
      • Inverted commas: the quotation marks that enclose the exact words in direct speech.
      • Conjunction "that": the linking word used before a reported statement in indirect speech.
      • Reporting clause: the part that contains the reporting verb and its subject.
      • Pronoun change: the change of I, we, you, he, she and they according to the speaker and the listener.
      • Tense back-shift: the moving back of the tense of the reported speech by one step when the reporting verb is in the past.
      • Time and place words: words such as now, today, tomorrow, here and this that change in indirect speech.

      Core Concepts and Rules

      • In direct speech the reporting verb is followed by a comma and the inverted commas; in indirect speech the comma and the inverted commas are removed and "that" is added for statements.
      • When the reporting verb is in the present or future tense, the tense of the reported speech does not change, as in She says that she is busy.
      • When the reporting verb is in the past tense, the reported speech moves one step back: simple present becomes simple past, present continuous becomes past continuous, present perfect and simple past become past perfect, and past perfect stays past perfect.
      • The future forms change as well: will and shall become would and should, can becomes could, and may becomes might.
      • The pronoun is changed by the rule that the first person follows the subject, the second person follows the object and the third person stays unchanged.
      • The reporting verb "say to" changes to "told", while "say" remains "said" and "said to" becomes "told".
      • Words of time and place change in a fixed way: now becomes then, today becomes that day, tomorrow becomes the next day, yesterday becomes the previous day, last night becomes the previous night, here becomes there, this becomes that and these become those.
      • Universal truths, habitual facts and proverbs do not change their tense even when the reporting verb is in the past, as in He said that the earth revolves around the sun.
      • Yes or no questions take "if" or "whether" and the word order becomes that of a statement, as in He asked if I liked tea.
      • Wh-questions keep the question word but change to statement order, as in She asked where I lived.
      • Imperative sentences change the reporting verb to ordered, told, requested, advised or forbade, and the command verb takes "to" before it, as in He told me to go.
      • Exclamatory sentences change the reporting verb to exclaimed with joy, sorrow or wonder, and the exclamation words are dropped, as in He exclaimed with joy that they had won.
      • Optative sentences, which express a wish or a prayer, change the reporting verb to wished or prayed, as in She wished that I might live long.

      Types of Narration Questions

      The first type gives a sentence in direct speech and asks for its indirect form, or the reverse. The second type tests a single rule, such as the change of a time word or the change of a modal. The third type tests questions, where "if" or "whether" and the word order must be correct. The fourth type tests imperatives and requests, where the reporting verb and "to" matter. The fifth type tests exclamatory and optative sentences, and the sixth type is error spotting, in which a wrong pronoun or a wrong tense in the reported clause has to be found and corrected.

      Steps to Solve a Narration Question

      First, identify the type of sentence - statement, question, imperative, exclamatory or optative - because the reporting verb depends on it. Second, remove the comma and the inverted commas and add the correct conjunction. Third, change the pronouns by the subject-object rule. Fourth, change the tense of the reported speech by one step back if the reporting verb is in the past, keeping universal truths unchanged. Fifth, change the words of time and place. Sixth, adjust the reporting verb itself, as say to becomes told. Finally, read the whole sentence to check that the tense, the pronouns and the word order are correct and that the meaning is unchanged.

      Examination Trends

      Narration is a reliable scoring topic because the rules are fixed and repeat every year. The examiner most often tests the tense back-shift with will, can and may, the change of time words such as tomorrow and yesterday, the "if" of yes or no questions and the "to" of imperatives. Error spotting usually hides a pronoun that has not changed or a reported clause that has kept the present tense. Exclamatory sentences with "Hurrah" and "Alas" also appear, and a candidate who identifies the sentence type first rarely loses marks.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Do not change the tense of a universal truth or a habitual fact.
      • Do not keep the pronoun unchanged; apply the subject-object rule every time.
      • Do not use "that" before a question or an imperative.
      • Do not forget to change "say to" into "told".
      • Do not keep the question word order in indirect questions; use statement order.
      • Do not change the tense when the reporting verb is in the present or future tense.

      How to Prepare This Topic

      Start by writing the tense back-shift table on a card, from simple present to past perfect, and learn the modal changes will to would, can to could and may to might. Then make a second card for the pronoun rule and a third for the changes of time and place, because these three cards together solve most of the questions. After that, take ten sentences of each type - statement, question, imperative and exclamatory - and change them one by one, checking the reporting verb and the conjunction each time.

      A useful habit is to report a short conversation you hear or read, first in direct speech and then in indirect speech, keeping the exact words in your mind while you change only the form. This makes the rules practical instead of mechanical. Keep a personal list of the words you forget to change, such as tomorrow, here and this, and revise that list before every test. Twenty narration sentences a day, in English and Assamese, builds speed and accuracy within two weeks and also strengthens tense and pronoun use throughout the English paper.

      Quick Revision Tips

      • Statements take "that"; questions take "if" or "whether"; imperatives take "to".
      • Move the tense one step back only when the reporting verb is in the past.
      • The first person follows the subject, the second the object, and the third stays unchanged.
      • Change now to then, tomorrow to the next day, here to there and this to that.
      • "Say to" becomes "told".
      • Never change a universal truth.

      Final Revision Note

      In the final revision, take two sentences of each type and change them from direct to indirect speech, then change two back. Check every pronoun, every tense and every time word, and note only the slips you repeat. The examiner tests whether you can report the meaning of a sentence without changing it, and a steady daily habit of narration practice makes this topic one of the fastest and safest in the English paper, while also improving tense, pronouns and sentence structure.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What is narration in English grammar?

      Narration is the way of reporting a speaker's words, either in the speaker's own words (direct speech) or in our own words (indirect speech).

      What changes when direct speech becomes indirect speech?

      The comma and inverted commas are removed, the reporting verb, pronouns, tense and words of time and place change, and a suitable conjunction such as "that", "if" or "to" is added.

      When does the tense not change in narration?

      The tense does not change when the reporting verb is in the present or future tense, or when the reported speech states a universal truth, a habitual fact or a proverb.

      How do questions change in indirect speech?

      Yes or no questions take "if" or "whether" and statement order, while wh-questions keep the question word but also use statement order.

      How are imperatives changed into indirect speech?

      The reporting verb changes to ordered, told, requested or advised, and the command verb is joined with "to", as in "Close the door" becoming "He told me to close the door".

      • চিৰন্তন সত্য, অভ্যাসগত সত্য আৰু প্ৰবচন reporting verb অতীতত থাকিলেও tense সলনি নহয়, যেনে He said that the earth revolves around the sun।
      • Yes/no প্ৰশ্নত "if" বা "whether" লয় আৰু শব্দৰ ক্ৰম statement ৰ দৰে হয়, যেনে He asked if I liked tea।
      • Wh-প্ৰশ্নত প্ৰশ্নবোধক শব্দ থাকে কিন্তু শব্দৰ ক্ৰম statement ৰ দৰে হয়, যেনে She asked where I lived।
      • Imperative বাক্যত reporting verb ordered, told, requested, advised বা forbade হয়, আৰু ক্ৰিয়াৰ আগত "to" আহে, যেনে He told me to go।
      • Exclamatory বাক্যত reporting verb exclaimed with joy, sorrow বা wonder হয়, আৰু বিস্ময়বোধক শব্দ আতৰোৱা হয়, যেনে He exclaimed with joy that they had won।
      • Optative বাক্যত, যিয়ে ইচ্ছা বা প্ৰাৰ্থনা সূচায়, reporting verb wished বা prayed হয়, যেনে She wished that I might live long।

      প্ৰশ্নৰ প্ৰকাৰ

      প্ৰথম প্ৰকাৰত direct speech ৰ এটা বাক্য দি indirect ৰূপ, বা ওলোটা বিচৰা হয়। দ্বিতীয় প্ৰকাৰত এটা নিয়ম, যেনে সময়বোধক শব্দ বা modal ৰ সলনি পৰীক্ষা কৰা হয়। তৃতীয় প্ৰকাৰত প্ৰশ্ন পৰীক্ষা কৰা হয়, য'ত "if" বা "whether" আৰু শব্দৰ ক্ৰম শুদ্ধ হ'ব লাগে। চতুৰ্থ প্ৰকাৰত imperative আৰু অনুৰোধ, য'ত reporting verb আৰু "to" গুৰুত্বপূৰ্ণ। পঞ্চম প্ৰকাৰত exclamatory আৰু optative বাক্য, আৰু ষষ্ঠ প্ৰকাৰত error spotting, য'ত reported clause ত ভুল pronoun বা ভুল tense বিচাৰি শুধৰাব লাগে।

      সমাধানৰ স্তৰ

      প্ৰথমে বাক্যটোৰ প্ৰকাৰ চিনাক্ত কৰক - statement, question, imperative, exclamatory নে optative - কাৰণ reporting verb ইয়াৰ ওপৰত নিৰ্ভৰ কৰে। দ্বিতীয়ে comma আৰু inverted comma আতৰাই শুদ্ধ conjunction যোগ কৰক। তৃতীয়ে কৰ্তা-কৰ্মৰ নিয়মেৰে pronoun সলনি কৰক। চতুৰ্থে reporting verb অতীতত থাকিলে reported speech ৰ tense এটা পদ পিছুৱাওক, চিৰন্তন সত্য অপৰিৱৰ্তিত ৰাখি। পঞ্চমে সময়-স্থান সূচক শব্দ সলনি কৰক। ষষ্ঠে reporting verb নিজেই শুধৰাওক, যেনে say to → told। শেষত গোটেই বাক্য পঢ়ি tense, pronoun আৰু শব্দৰ ক্ৰম শুদ্ধ আৰু অৰ্থ অপৰিৱৰ্তিত নে নিশ্চিত হওক।

      পৰীক্ষাৰ প্ৰৱণতা

      Narration এটা নিৰ্ভৰযোগ্য নম্বৰ দিয়া বিষয় কাৰণ নিয়মবোৰ নিৰ্দিষ্ট আৰু প্ৰতি বছৰে পুনৰাবৃত্তি হয়। পৰীক্ষকে সৰ্বাধিক will, can আৰু may ৰ tense back-shift, tomorrow আৰু yesterday ৰ সলনি, yes/no প্ৰশ্নৰ "if" আৰু imperative ৰ "to" পৰীক্ষা কৰে। Error spotting ত প্ৰায়ে সলনি নোহোৱা pronoun বা present tense ৰাখি দিয়া reported clause লুকুৱাই ৰখা হয়। "Hurrah" আৰু "Alas" থকা exclamatory বাক্যও আহে, আৰু প্ৰথমে বাক্যৰ প্ৰকাৰ চিনি পোৱা প্ৰাৰ্থীয়ে কদাচিৎ নম্বৰ হেৰুৱায়।

      সাধাৰণ ভুল

      • চিৰন্তন সত্য বা অভ্যাসগত সত্যৰ tense সলনি নকৰিব।
      • Pronoun অপৰিৱৰ্তিত নেৰাখিব; প্ৰতিবাৰ কৰ্তা-কৰ্মৰ নিয়ম প্ৰয়োগ কৰক।
      • প্ৰশ্ন বা imperative ৰ আগত "that" নিদিব।
      • "say to" ক "told" লৈ সলনি কৰিবলৈ নাপাহৰিব।
      • Indirect প্ৰশ্নত প্ৰশ্নবোধক শব্দৰ ক্ৰম নাৰাখি statement ৰ ক্ৰম ব্যৱহাৰ কৰক।
      • Reporting verb বৰ্তমান বা ভৱিষ্যত কালত থাকিলে tense সলনি নকৰিব।

      প্ৰস্তুতি কেনেকৈ কৰিব

      আৰম্ভণিতে simple present ৰ পৰা past perfect লৈকে tense back-shift ৰ এটা টেবুল কাৰ্ডত লিখক, আৰু will → would, can → could, may → might modal ৰ সলনি শিকক। তাৰ পিছত pronoun নিয়মৰ বাবে দ্বিতীয়খন কাৰ্ড আৰু সময়-স্থান সলনিৰ বাবে তৃতীয়খন কাৰ্ড বনাওক, কাৰণ এই তিনি খন কাৰ্ডে বেছিভাগ প্ৰশ্ন সমাধান কৰে। ইয়াৰ পিছত প্ৰতিটো প্ৰকাৰৰ - statement, question, imperative আৰু exclamatory - দহটা বাক্য লৈ এটা এটাকৈ সলনি কৰক, প্ৰতিবাৰ reporting verb আৰু conjunction পৰীক্ষা কৰি।

      এটা উপযোগী অভ্যাস হ'ল - শুনা বা পঢ়া এটা সৰু কথা-বতৰা প্ৰথমে direct speech আৰু তাৰ পিছত indirect speech ত ৰিপ'ৰ্ট কৰা, কেৱল ৰূপ সলনি কৰি অৰ্থ মনত ৰাখি। ইয়ে নিয়মবোৰ যান্ত্ৰিক নহৈ প্ৰায়োগিক কৰি তোলে। সলনি কৰিবলৈ পাহৰা শব্দ, যেনে tomorrow, here আৰু this, ৰ এটা ব্যক্তিগত তালিকা ৰাখি প্ৰতিটো পৰীক্ষাৰ আগতে পুনৰালোচনা কৰক। ইংৰাজী আৰু অসমীয়াত দৈনিক বিশটা narration বাক্যই দুসপ্তাহৰ ভিতৰতে দ্ৰুতি আৰু শুদ্ধতা গঢ়ে আৰু গোটেই ইংৰাজী পত্ৰত tense আৰু pronoun ৰ ব্যৱহাৰো সবল কৰে।

      দ্ৰুত পুনৰালোচনাৰ টিপছ

      • Statement এ "that", প্ৰশ্নই "if"/"whether", imperative এ "to" লয়।
      • Reporting verb অতীতত থাকিলেহে tense এটা পদ পিছুৱাই যায়।
      • প্ৰথম পুৰুষ কৰ্তাৰ, দ্বিতীয় কৰ্মৰ, আৰু তৃতীয় অপৰিৱৰ্তিত।
      • now → then, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that।
      • "Say to" → "told"।
      • চিৰন্তন সত্য কেতিয়াও সলনি নকৰিব।

      শেষ পুনৰালোচনা

      শেষ পুনৰালোচনাত প্ৰতিটো প্ৰকাৰৰ দুটাকৈ বাক্য direct ৰ পৰা indirect লৈ সলনি কৰক, তাৰ পিছত দুটা ওলোটাকৈ সলনি কৰক। প্ৰতিটো pronoun, tense আৰু সময়বোধক শব্দ পৰীক্ষা কৰি কেৱল বাৰে বাৰে কৰা ভুলবোৰ টুকি লওক। পৰীক্ষকে অৰ্থ নসলোৱাকৈ বাক্যটো ৰিপ'ৰ্ট কৰিব পাৰে নে নাই সেয়াই পৰীক্ষা কৰে, আৰু narration ৰ নিত্য অভ্যাসে এই বিষয়টো ইংৰাজী পত্ৰৰ আটাইতকৈ দ্ৰুত আৰু নিৰাপদ এটা কৰি তোলে, লগতে tense, pronoun আৰু sentence structure উন্নত কৰে।

      সঘনাই সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

      ইংৰাজী ব্যাকৰণত narration কাক বোলে?

      Narration হৈছে বক্তাৰ কথা ৰিপ'ৰ্ট কৰাৰ ধৰণ — হয় বক্তাৰ নিজৰ শব্দত (direct speech), নাইবা নিজৰ ভাষাত (indirect speech)।

      Direct speech ৰ পৰা indirect speech লৈ যাওঁতে কি কি সলনি হয়?

      Comma আৰু inverted comma আতৰোৱা হয়, reporting verb, pronoun, tense আৰু সময়-স্থান শব্দ সলনি হয়, আৰু "that", "if" বা "to" ধৰণৰ উপযুক্ত conjunction যোগ কৰা হয়।

      Narration ত কেতিয়া tense সলনি নহয়?

      Reporting verb বৰ্তমান বা ভৱিষ্যত কালত থাকিলে, নাইবা reported speech এ চিৰন্তন সত্য, অভ্যাসগত সত্য বা প্ৰবচন ক'লে tense সলনি নহয়।

      Indirect speech ত প্ৰশ্ন কেনেকৈ সলনি হয়?

      Yes/no প্ৰশ্নই "if" বা "whether" আৰু statement ৰ ক্ৰম লয়, আনহাতে wh-প্ৰশ্নই প্ৰশ্নবোধক শব্দ ৰাখিও statement ৰ ক্ৰম ব্যৱহাৰ কৰে।

      Imperative বাক্য indirect speech লৈ কেনেকৈ সলনি হয়?

      Reporting verb ordered, told, requested বা advised হয়, আৰু ক্ৰিয়াৰ লগত "to" যোগ হয়, যেনে "Close the door" → "He told me to close the door"।
