      Parts of Speech: Types, Rules and Examples

      A part of speech is the class or category to which a word belongs according to the work it does in a sentence. English has eight traditional parts of speech - noun, pronoun, adjective, verb, adverb, preposition, conjunction and interjection - and articles and determiners are usually treated as a special kind of adjective. The same word can belong to different parts of speech in different sentences, so a part of speech is decided by the job a word does, not by its spelling. The word "book", for example, is a noun in "I bought a book" but a verb in "Please book the ticket".

      Parts of speech is one of the highest-scoring topics in the English section of APSC, ADRE, Assam Police, DHS, DME, SSC and Railway examinations, and it is also the base on which every other grammar topic - tense, subject-verb agreement, voice, narration and error spotting - is built. A candidate who can identify the part of speech of a word instantly can usually correct a sentence without reciting the whole rule. This article gives a complete deep-study revision of the topic: the basic terms, the core rules, the kinds of questions asked, the step-by-step method, the common mistakes and a practical preparation plan.

      Basic Terms

      • Noun: a word that names a person, place, thing, idea or feeling, such as "Assam", "teacher", "table" or "honesty".
      • Pronoun: a word used in place of a noun, such as he, she, it, they, who and which.
      • Adjective: a word that describes or qualifies a noun or pronoun, such as "tall", "three" or "beautiful".
      • Verb: a word that shows an action, an event or a state of being, such as "run", "is" or "seems".
      • Adverb: a word that modifies a verb, an adjective or another adverb, such as "quickly", "very" or "always".
      • Preposition: a word that shows the relation of a noun or pronoun to another word, such as in, on, at, under and between.
      • Conjunction: a word that joins words, phrases or clauses, such as and, but, or, because and although.
      • Interjection: a word that expresses sudden feeling, such as "Oh!", "Alas!" and "Hurrah!".
      • Article: a, an or the, used before a noun to show whether it is definite or indefinite.
      • Determiner: a word such as this, some, any, each or every that comes before a noun and limits it.
      • Proper noun: the special name of a particular person, place or thing, always written with a capital letter, such as Ganga or Monday.
      • Abstract noun: the name of a quality, action or state that cannot be seen, such as courage, childhood or freedom.

      Core Concepts and Rules

      • The part of speech of a word depends on its function in a sentence, so the same word can change class from sentence to sentence.
      • A noun can be common, proper, collective, material or abstract, and each kind behaves differently with articles and verbs.
      • A pronoun must agree with its noun in number, gender and person; "everybody" is singular even though it seems to refer to many people.
      • An adjective can be attributive (before the noun) or predicative (after a linking verb), as in "a happy child" and "the child is happy".
      • An adverb often ends in -ly, but not always; "fast", "hard" and "well" are adverbs without -ly, and "friendly" is an adjective.
      • Prepositions are followed by a noun, a pronoun or a gerund, never by the base form of a verb: we say "good at swimming", not "good at swim".
      • A conjunction joins units of the same grammatical rank, while a subordinating conjunction such as "because" introduces a dependent clause.
      • Articles are decided by sound, not spelling: "an hour" and "a university" depend on the first sound of the following word.
      • Determiners include articles, demonstratives, possessives, quantifiers and numbers, and only one central determiner can come before a noun.
      • Many words change their part of speech with a suffix: "-ness" makes a noun, "-ful" makes an adjective and "-ly" usually makes an adverb.

      Types of Parts of Speech Questions

      The first type asks the candidate to name the part of speech of an underlined word. The second type gives a sentence with a blank and asks which kind of word is missing. The third type gives four words and asks which one is a different part of speech from the other three. The fourth type asks the candidate to find a word used as a particular part of speech, such as an adjective used as a noun. The fifth type is error spotting, where a wrong article, pronoun or preposition has to be corrected, and the sixth type asks for the correct form of a word - noun, verb, adjective or adverb - to fill a blank.

      Steps to Solve a Parts of Speech Question

      First, read the whole sentence and find the word that is underlined or missing. Second, ask what job that word is doing: is it naming, replacing a name, describing, showing action, modifying, relating, joining or expressing feeling? Third, look at the words on either side, because a word before a noun is often an adjective or determiner and a word after a verb is often an adverb or an object. Fourth, check number, gender and person if the word is a pronoun, and check the first sound if the word is an article. Fifth, test each option by placing it in the sentence and reading the sentence aloud. Finally, choose the option that makes the sentence grammatically correct and natural.

      Examination Trends

      In competitive examinations parts of speech carries two to five marks, usually mixed with error spotting and fill in the blanks. Questions on articles, pronouns and prepositions are the most frequent, and questions that hide a change of part of speech - a noun used as a verb, or an adjective used as a noun - appear regularly to test careful reading. Because the same rules are used in voice, narration and subject-verb agreement, this topic gives value far beyond its own marks, and a candidate who masters it saves time across the whole English paper.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Do not decide a part of speech by the spelling of a word; decide it by the work the word does in the sentence.
      • Do not call every word ending in -ly an adverb; some, such as "friendly" and "lovely", are adjectives.
      • Do not use a preposition before the base form of a verb; use a gerund instead.
      • Do not treat "everybody", "someone" and "each" as plural; they take singular verbs.
      • Do not choose an article by the letter a word starts with; choose it by the sound.
      • Do not mix up a demonstrative adjective and a demonstrative pronoun, or a possessive adjective and a possessive pronoun.

      How to Prepare This Topic

      Begin by making a chart of the eight parts of speech and writing three examples of each from your own daily reading. Then take one sentence at a time and label every word, because this active labelling trains the eye to see the function of a word. After that, move to mixed exercises of error spotting and fill in the blanks, where the part of speech is not named but has to be discovered.

      A very useful habit is to keep a small notebook of words that changed their part of speech in the questions you got wrong, such as "water" as a noun and as a verb, and to revise that list every week. Another good method is to read one editorial or one news report each day and underline every adjective and adverb, because most mistakes in the English section are mistakes of description and degree. Daily practice of twenty questions for a fortnight, in both English and Assamese, makes the topic quick and accurate.

      Quick Revision Tips

      • Label every word in a sentence to train the eye to see function.
      • Remember the eight parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction and interjection.
      • Articles are decided by sound: "an hour", "a university", "the MLA".
      • A pronoun must match its noun in number, gender and person.
      • A preposition is followed by a noun, a pronoun or a gerund.
      • Revise the -ness, -ful and -ly suffixes that build nouns, adjectives and adverbs.

      Final Revision Note

      In the final revision, take ten mixed sentences and label every word, then correct one article, one pronoun and one preposition mistake in each. Note down the errors you repeat and redrill only those. Clear thinking about the function of a word is the real skill, and with regular practice parts of speech becomes one of the easiest and most useful topics in the English paper, helping not only its own questions but also voice, narration, tense and error spotting.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What is a part of speech?

      A part of speech is the class to which a word belongs according to the work it does in a sentence, such as noun, pronoun, adjective, verb, adverb, preposition, conjunction or interjection.

      Can one word be more than one part of speech?

      Yes. A word's part of speech depends on its function, so "book" is a noun in "a book" and a verb in "to book a seat".

      How are articles and determiners related to parts of speech?

      Articles (a, an, the) and determiners (this, some, any, each) are usually treated as a special kind of adjective because they come before a noun and limit it.

      Why is parts of speech important for competitive exams?

      It is the base of grammar, so it directly helps in error spotting, fill in the blanks, voice, narration and subject-verb agreement questions in APSC, ADRE and Assam Police papers.

      How many parts of speech are there in English?

      • Noun (বস্তু-নাম): ব্যক্তি, বস্তু, স্থান, ভাব বা অৱস্থাৰ নাম, যেনে "Assam", "teacher", "table", "honesty"।

      • Adjective (বিশেষণ): noun বা pronoun-ক বৰ্ণনা কৰা শব্দ, যেনে "tall", "three", "beautiful"।
      • Verb (ক্ৰিয়া): কাম, ঘটনা বা অৱস্থা সূচোৱা শব্দ, যেনে "run", "is", "seems"।
      • Adverb (ক্ৰিয়াৰ বিশেষণ): verb, adjective বা আন adverb-ক সলনি কৰা শব্দ, যেনে "quickly", "very", "always"।
      • Preposition (সম্বন্ধবোধক): দুটা শব্দৰ সম্বন্ধ দেখুওৱা শব্দ, যেনে in, on, at, under, between।
      • Conjunction (সংযোজক): শব্দ, পদ বা খণ্ডবাক্য জোৰা লগোৱা শব্দ, যেনে and, but, or, because, although।

      • বাক্যত শব্দৰ কামেহে part of speech নিৰ্ণয় কৰে, গতিকে একে শব্দই বেলেগ বাক্যত বেলেগ শ্ৰেণী ল'ব পাৰে।

      • Pronoun-এ নিজৰ noun-ৰ সৈতে সংখ্যা, লিংগ আৰু পুৰুষত মিল থাকিব লাগে; "everybody" দেখাত বহুতক বুজালেও একবচন।

      • Conjunction-এ একে মানৰ পদ জোৰা লগায়; "because" আদি subordinating conjunction-এ নিৰ্ভৰ খণ্ডবাক্য আৰম্ভ কৰে।
      • Article ধ্বনিৰ ভিত্তিত নিৰ্ণয় হয়, বানানৰ নহয়; "an hour", "a university" - এই দুয়ো প্ৰথম ধ্বনিৰ ওপৰত নিৰ্ভৰশীল।
      • Determiner-ৰ ভিতৰত article, demonstrative, possessive, quantifier আৰু সংখ্যা থাকে; noun-ৰ আগত মাত্ৰ এটা central determiner আহিব পাৰে।
      • বহুত শব্দই suffix-ৰ সহায়ত শ্ৰেণী সলায়: "-ness" noun, "-ful" adjective আৰু "-ly" প্ৰায়ে adverb বনায়।

      প্ৰশ্নৰ প্ৰকাৰ

      প্ৰথম প্ৰকাৰত তলত দিয়া বা দাগি দিয়া শব্দটো কোনটো part of speech সোধা হয়। দ্বিতীয় প্ৰকাৰত এটা খালী ঠাই দি কোন শ্ৰেণীৰ শব্দ প্ৰয়োজন সোধা হয়। তৃতীয় প্ৰকাৰত চাৰিটা শব্দ দি কোনটো আন তিনিটাৰ পৰা পৃথক সোধা হয়। চতুৰ্থ প্ৰকাৰত কোনো এটা শব্দ কিহিচাপে ব্যৱহাৰ হৈছে (যেনে adjective-ক noun হিচাপে) বিচৰা হয়। পঞ্চম প্ৰকাৰ হৈছে error spotting, য'ত ভুল article, pronoun বা preposition শুধৰাব লাগে; ষষ্ঠ প্ৰকাৰত খালী ঠাই পূৰাবলৈ শব্দৰ শুদ্ধ ৰূপ - noun, verb, adjective বা adverb - বিচৰা হয়।

      সমাধানৰ স্তৰ

      প্ৰথমে গোটেই বাক্য পঢ়ি দাগি দিয়া বা অনুপস্থিত শব্দটো চিনি লওক। দ্বিতীয়ে, সেই শব্দই কি কাম কৰিছে সোধক: নাম দিছে, নামৰ সলনি আহিছে, বৰ্ণনা দিছে, ক্ৰিয়া দেখুৱাইছে, সলনি কৰিছে, সম্বন্ধ জুড়িছে, সংযোগ কৰিছে নে ভাৱ প্ৰকাশ কৰিছে? তৃতীয়ে, দুই কাষৰ শব্দ চাওক, কাৰণ noun-ৰ আগত অহা শব্দ প্ৰায়ে adjective নাইবা determiner, আনহাতে verb-ৰ পিছত অহা শব্দ প্ৰায়ে adverb বা object। চতুৰ্থে, pronoun হ'লে সংখ্যা, লিংগ আৰু পুৰুষ, আৰু article হ'লে প্ৰথম ধ্বনি পৰীক্ষা কৰক। পঞ্চমে, প্ৰতিটো বিকল্প বাক্যত বহুৱাই পঢ়ি চাওক। শেষত, যিটোৱে বাক্যটোক ব্যাকৰণমতে শুদ্ধ আৰু স্বাভাৱিক কৰে সেইটো বাছনি কৰক।

      পৰীক্ষাৰ প্ৰৱণতা

      প্ৰতিযোগিতামূলক পৰীক্ষাত parts of speech-এ দুইৰ পৰা পাঁচ নম্বৰ বহন কৰে, সাধাৰণতে error spotting আৰু fill in the blanks-ৰ লগত মিহলি হৈ। article, pronoun আৰু preposition-ৰ প্ৰশ্ন আটাইতকৈ বেছি আহে, আৰু শব্দৰ শ্ৰেণী সলনি কৰা প্ৰশ্ন (noun-ক verb হিচাপে, adjective-ক noun হিচাপে) নিয়মীয়াকৈ আহে যাতে প্ৰাৰ্থীৰ সূক্ষ্ম পঢ়াৰ ক্ষমতা পৰীক্ষা হয়। একে নিয়ম voice, narration আৰু subject-verb agreement-তো লগা বাবে এই বিষয়ৰ মূল্য নিজৰ নম্বৰতকৈ বহুত বেছি, আৰু যিজনে ইয়াত দক্ষতা অৰ্জন কৰে তেওঁ গোটেই ইংৰাজী পত্ৰত সময় বচায়।

      সাধাৰণ ভুল

      • শব্দৰ বানান চাই part of speech নিৰ্ণয় নকৰিব; বাক্যত কৰা কামেৰে নিৰ্ণয় কৰক।
      • -ly-ৰে শেষ হোৱা প্ৰতিটো শব্দক adverb নক'ব; "friendly", "lovely" আদি adjective।
      • ক্ৰিয়াৰ মূল ৰূপৰ আগত preposition নিদিব; gerund ব্যৱহাৰ কৰক।
      • "everybody", "someone", "each"-ক বহুবচন নধৰিব; এইবোৰ একবচন verb লয়।
      • শব্দৰ প্ৰথম আখৰ চাই article নুবাচিব; ধ্বনি চাই বাছক।
      • Demonstrative adjective আৰু demonstrative pronoun, বা possessive adjective আৰু possessive pronoun মিলাই নিদিব।

      প্ৰস্তুতি কেনেকৈ কৰিব

      আৰম্ভণিতে আঠটা part of speech-ৰ এটা চাৰ্ট বনাই প্ৰতিটোৰ তিনি উদাহৰণ নিজৰ দৈনন্দিন পঢ়াৰ পৰা লিখক। তাৰ পিছত এটা এটা বাক্য লৈ প্ৰতিটো শব্দ চিহ্নিত কৰক, কাৰণ এনে সক্ৰিয় চিহ্নিতকৰণে শব্দৰ কাম চিনি পোৱাৰ অভ্যাস গঢ়ে। ইয়াৰ পিছত error spotting আৰু fill in the blanks-ৰ মিহলি অভ্যাসলৈ যাওঁক, য'ত শ্ৰেণীটোৰ নাম নকৈ আৱিষ্কাৰ কৰিব লাগে।

      এটা বৰ উপযোগী অভ্যাস হ'ল - যিবোৰ প্ৰশ্নত ভুল কৰিছে সেইবোৰত শ্ৰেণী সলোৱা শব্দৰ এটা সৰু টোকাবহী ৰাখক, যেনে "water" noun হিচাপে আৰু verb হিচাপে, আৰু প্ৰতি সপ্তাহে সেই তালিকা পুনৰাবৃত্তি কৰক। আন এটা ভাল পদ্ধতি হ'ল - দৈনিক এটা সম্পাদকীয় বা বাতৰি পঢ়ি প্ৰতিটো adjective আৰু adverb তলত দাগ দিয়ক, কাৰণ ইংৰাজী অংশৰ বেছিভাগ ভুল নৱবৰ্ণনাৰ ভুল। অসমীয়া আৰু ইংৰাজী দুয়ো ভাষাত দৈনিক বিশটা প্ৰশ্ন দুসপ্তাহ অভ্যাস কৰিলে বিষয়টো দ্ৰুত আৰু সঠিক হৈ পৰে।

      দ্ৰুত পুনৰালোচনাৰ টিপছ

      • প্ৰতিটো শব্দ চিহ্নিত কৰি কাম চিনি পোৱাৰ অভ্যাস কৰক।
      • নিয়ম মনত ৰাখক: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection।
      • Article ধ্বনিৰ দ্বাৰা নিৰ্ণয় হয়: "an hour", "a university", "the MLA"।
      • Pronoun-এ সংখ্যা, লিংগ আৰু পুৰুষত noun-ৰ সৈতে মিলিব লাগে।
      • Preposition-ৰ পিছত noun, pronoun বা gerund আহে।
      • -ness, -ful, -ly suffix-এ noun, adjective, adverb বনায়।

      শেষ পুনৰালোচনা

      শেষ পুনৰালোচনাত দহটা মিহলি বাক্য লৈ প্ৰতিটো শব্দ চিহ্নিত কৰক, তাৰ পিছত প্ৰতিটো বাক্যত এটা article, এটা pronoun আৰু এটা preposition-ৰ ভুল শুধৰাওক। যিবোৰ ভুল বাৰে বাৰে কৰে সেইবোৰ টুকি লৈ কেৱল সেইবোৰ অভ্যাস কৰক। শব্দৰ কাম সম্পৰ্কে স্পষ্ট ধাৰণাই প্ৰকৃত দক্ষতা, আৰু নিয়মীয়া অভ্যাসত parts of speech ইংৰাজী পত্ৰৰ এটা আটাইতকৈ সহজ আৰু উপযোগী বিষয় হৈ পৰে, যিয়ে নিজৰ প্ৰশ্নৰ লগতে voice, narration, tense আৰু error spotting-তো সহায় কৰে।

      সঘনাই সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

      Part of speech কাক বোলে?

      বাক্যত এটা শব্দই কৰা কামৰ ভিত্তিত সেই শব্দক যি শ্ৰেণীত ধৰা হয়, তাক part of speech বোলে. যেনে noun, pronoun, adjective, verb, adverb, preposition, conjunction বা interjection।

      এটা শব্দয়ে একাধিক part of speech হ'ব পাৰেনে?

      হয়। শব্দৰ কামেহে শ্ৰেণী ঠিক কৰে, গতিকে "book" শব্দটো "a book" ত noun আৰু "to book a seat" ত verb।

      Article আৰু determiner-ৰ সম্পৰ্ক কি?

      Article (a, an, the) আৰু determiner (this, some, any, each) দুয়ো noun-ৰ আগত আহি তাৰ পৰিসৰ নিৰ্ধাৰণ কৰে, সেয়ে ইহঁতক adjective-ৰ এক বিশেষ ৰূপ হিচাপে ধৰা হয়।

      প্ৰতিযোগিতামূলক পৰীক্ষাৰ বাবে parts of speech কিয় গুৰুত্বপূৰ্ণ?

      ই ব্যাকৰণৰ ভিত্তি, গতিকে APSC, ADRE আৰু অসম আৰক্ষীৰ error spotting, fill in the blanks, voice, narration আৰু subject-verb agreement আদি প্ৰশ্নত পোনপটীয়াকৈ সহায় কৰে।

      ইংৰাজীত কিমানটা part of speech আছে?

      পৰম্পৰাগতভাৱে আঠটা: noun, pronoun, adjective, verb, adverb, preposition, conjunction আৰু interjection।
