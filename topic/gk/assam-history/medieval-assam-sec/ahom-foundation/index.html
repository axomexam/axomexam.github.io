    About the Foundation of the Ahom Kingdom — Sukapha and the Tai Arrival

    The Ahom kingdom was founded by Sukapha, a prince of the Tai (Shan) people who came from the Tai principality of Mong Mao in the upper Irrawaddy region. His father was Chang-Nyeu. In 1228 AD Sukapha crossed the Patkai mountain range, entering the Brahmaputra valley from the east. This is the accepted traditional founding date of the Ahom kingdom, and the dynasty he established came to be known as the Ahom dynasty.

    Rather than fight at once, Sukapha first settled in the valley and built friendly relations with the local Moran and Borahi tribes. He established his first capital at Charaideo in 1253 AD, in the eastern part of the valley. Charaideo later became famous as the sacred burial ground of the Ahom kings, where the royal Maidams (burial mounds) were raised. Sukapha died in 1268 AD, leaving behind a kingdom that would grow into one of the longest-lasting dynasties in Indian history.

    The name Sukapha means Tiger prince: “su” carries the sense of exalted or great, while “kha” means tiger in the Tai language, and so the full title Chaolung Sukapha means the great lord. Tradition holds that about 9,000 followers accompanied him from Mong Mao. The early Ahoms followed animism, worshipping spirits and nature, and only in later times did the dynasty adopt Hinduism. This long religious transition is itself an important part of Ahom history.

    Sukapha's foundation matters because it marks the beginning of Ahom rule in Assam. From a small settlement at Charaideo, the Ahoms went on to dominate the Brahmaputra valley for nearly six hundred years, and their statecraft, language and culture shaped the history of the whole region.
