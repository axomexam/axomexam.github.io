      Search on axomexam - Find the Right Question in Seconds

      The search page lets you jump straight to a topic, a question or a study note without browsing through menus. With thousands of bilingual questions organised under hundreds of topics, knowing how to search well saves a surprising amount of study time. This guide explains what you can search for and how to phrase a query so that the exact topic appears on top.

      What you can search

      The search covers every topic, question, answer and explanation available on axomexam, along with previous year sections and study notes. You can type an exam name such as ADRE or Assam Police, a subject such as polity or arithmetic, or a specific idea such as fundamental rights, blood relations or the Brahmaputra. Results update as you type, so you do not need to press a button or wait for a page to load.

      How to get the best results

      Use the most distinctive word of the topic instead of a full sentence. Searching "saraighat" will find the battle topic faster than searching "battle that was fought in Assam". If your first keyword is too broad, add a second word, for example "saraighat 1671" or "saraighat ahom". Short, specific keywords match the topic titles and question text directly. If a query returns nothing, check the spelling or try the Assamese term, because several topics are indexed in both languages.

      Searching and practising together

      The fastest revision loop is to search a topic, read the answers, and then attempt the matching mock test or question set on the same subject. For example, search "fundamental rights", read the twenty-five questions and their answers, and then open the Polity mock test to check whether you can answer similar questions under a timer. This turns a passive search into active practice, which is what actually improves your score.

      Popular searches on the platform

      • ADRE previous year question paper and ADRE mock test.
      • Assam History, the Ahom dynasty and the Battle of Saraighat.
      • Fundamental rights, the Preamble and the Sixth Schedule.
      • Assam Geography, the Brahmaputra and Majuli.
      • Percentage, average and time and work for mathematics.
      • Coding-decoding and blood relations for reasoning.

      Useful habits

      Keep a notebook beside you while searching. When you find a fact you did not know, write it in your own words rather than copying it. Revise that notebook weekly. If you find a question whose answer or explanation seems wrong, use the contact page to report it, and the team will correct it. A clean and accurate question bank is useful only when learners report mistakes, so your feedback directly improves the platform.

      Searching in Assamese and English

      Every question on the platform exists in two languages, so a search for an English term such as "average" and a search for its Assamese equivalent both lead to the same concept. When a topic feels difficult in one language, search it in the other and compare the two versions side by side. This is especially helpful for candidates whose schooling was in Assamese but whose exam paper is in English, or the other way round. Reading the same fact twice, once in each language, strengthens recall far more than reading it twice in a single language.

      Start with one search now: pick your weakest subject, find its top topic and spend ten minutes on it. Small, focused search sessions add up quickly before an exam.
