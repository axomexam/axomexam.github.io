    Spelling Correction and Spotting Errors

    Spelling correction and error spotting form one of the most dependable scoring areas in the General English paper of ADRE Grade IV, APSC, Assam Police, SSC and Railway examinations. In this topic a sentence is given with either a wrongly spelt word or a grammatical mistake hidden inside it, and the candidate must find the part that contains the error. The good news is that this topic does not demand wide reading so much as a sharp eye for a few repeated patterns. Most of the errors that appear year after year belong to a small number of groups: spelling, subject-verb agreement, wrong preposition, wrong article, wrong tense, wrong pronoun and unnecessary repetition. Once a candidate learns these groups, the same mistakes can be spotted again and again in different sentences.

    What the Topic Actually Tests

    Two different skills are tested under one heading. The first is spelling, where a word is written in a way that is close to correct but not correct, such as recieve instead of receive. The second is grammatical accuracy, where every word is spelt correctly but the sentence breaks a rule of grammar, such as He go to school daily. In some papers one part of the sentence is marked as wrong and the candidate must say which part it is; in others four sentences are given and the candidate must choose the one that is fully correct. Both forms reward the same preparation, because both rest on knowing the standard rules of written English.

    The Common Spelling Rules

    Several spelling rules cover a large share of the words tested. First, the rule of i before e except after c: believe, chief and field, but receive, deceive and ceiling. Second, when a word ends in a consonant followed by y, the y changes to i before a suffix: happy becomes happiness, and carry becomes carried. Third, when a word ends in a silent e, the e is usually dropped before a vowel suffix: make becomes making, and write becomes writing. Fourth, when a short word ends in a single consonant after a single vowel, the last consonant is doubled before a suffix: run becomes running, and big becomes bigger. These four rules alone settle a large number of the spelling questions that come in the examination.

    Words with Similar Spellings

    Some errors arise because two correct words look almost the same and the candidate writes one for the other. Advice is a noun and advise is a verb; practice is a noun and practise is a verb; principal means the head of a school while principle means a rule or a truth; stationary means not moving while stationery means writing material; affect is a verb and effect is usually a noun; complement means to complete while compliment means to praise. In the same way desert and dessert, personal and personnel, later and latter, loose and lose, weather and whether are all pairs that appear in error spotting. Learning each pair together with its meaning is far safer than guessing from memory.

    Commonly Misspelt Words for the Examination

    The following words are frequently written wrongly in answer sheets and therefore appear in the paper: accommodation, occurrence, successful, embarrassment, environment, government, definitely, necessary, separate, address, committee, beginning, convenient, tomorrow, February, grammar, occasion, receive, restaurant, privilege, rhythm, tongue, vacuum, questionnaire, maintenance, business, calendar, conscience, courageous, excellence, forty, guarantee, height, jewellery, knowledge, leisure, library, marriage, medicine, neighbour, opportunity, parliament, possession, recommend, secretary, sincerely, surprise, temperature and unnecessary.

    Spotting Errors in a Sentence

    When a sentence is given with an error, the candidate should read it once from beginning to end without stopping, because the meaning often tells where the fault lies. Then each of the four parts should be checked against the rules of grammar in a fixed order: subject and verb first, then tense, then pronoun, then preposition, then article, and finally spelling. Taking the rules in the same order every time prevents the candidate from missing the error because of haste. If the sentence reads correctly but one word looks slightly unusual, the fault is usually in the spelling of that word.

    Subject-Verb Agreement Errors

    One of the most common grammatical mistakes is a mismatch between subject and verb. A singular subject takes a singular verb and a plural subject takes a plural verb. Errors such as The list of items are long, or One of my friend is coming, or Each of the boys were present break this rule. Words like along with, as well as and together with do not change the number of the subject, so the verb must still agree with the real subject. The candidate must ignore the words standing between the subject and the verb and match the two directly.

    Preposition and Article Errors

    Wrong prepositions are very hard to notice because a wrong preposition often sounds almost right. The candidate must therefore learn the fixed pairings: depend on, listen to, look after, good at, afraid of, angry with, married to, ashamed of and similar to. Articles also produce errors. a is used before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound, so an hour, a university and an MLA are correct. The definite article the is used before unique things and before the names of rivers, seas and holy books, but not before the names of most countries.

    Tense and Pronoun Errors

    Tense errors usually appear when two parts of a sentence are set in different times without reason, such as He came and tells me the news. The tense must remain consistent unless the meaning demands a change. Pronoun errors appear when the pronoun does not match the noun it stands for, such as Every student must bring their book, or when the case is wrong, such as Between you and I. The pronoun must agree with its noun in number, gender and person, and the correct case must be used after a preposition.

    Double Errors and How to Handle Them

    In a well-set paper, if the instruction says there is one error, there is only one error, even if the sentence looks suspicious in two places. The candidate must then choose the part that clearly breaks a rule and leave the part that merely sounds odd. Often a wrongly spelt word draws the eye, but the real error lies elsewhere. Reading the instruction carefully and trusting the most definite rule is the safest approach. Guessing a second error where none exists is a common way of losing an easy mark.

    Shortcut Methods for Quick Answers

    First, check the verb against the subject before anything else. Then look for a fixed preposition pairing, because a wrong preposition is the easiest error to hide. Next, scan for the articles a, an and the and test them against the sound rule. Then examine the tense of each clause and see whether the times agree. Finally, check the spelling of every long word. This fixed order turns a confusing sentence into a short checklist and saves precious time in the examination hall.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Finding an error where the sentence is actually correct.
    • Correcting the spelling when the real error is grammatical.
    • Missing a wrong preposition because it sounds natural.
    • Ignoring the real subject and matching the verb to a nearby noun.
    • Changing the tense of a sentence that is already consistent.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is error spotting the same as spelling correction?

    Not exactly. Spelling correction deals only with wrongly spelt words, while error spotting can involve any rule of grammar such as agreement, tense, preposition or article. Many papers combine the two under a single heading, so both must be prepared together.

    How do I know there is only one error?

    The instruction decides this. If it says there is one error, choose the part that clearly breaks a definite rule and do not invent a second fault. A sentence may sound awkward in two places, but only one will truly violate grammar.

    Why do wrong prepositions sound correct?

    Because in ordinary speech we hear many wrong pairings and get used to them. The safe method is to memorise the fixed pairs such as depend on, listen to and good at, and to test every preposition against that list.

    Which spelling rule is asked most often?

    The i before e rule and the doubling of the final consonant are the most frequent. The y to i change and the silent e rule also appear regularly, so all four should be learnt with examples.

    Can wide reading replace rule learning?

    Reading helps a great deal by building a feel for what is correct, but it cannot replace the rules in an examination where a single wrong letter matters. The best result comes from reading supported by a clear knowledge of the rules.

    ভালদৰে সজোৱা প্ৰশ্নপত্ৰত যদি নিৰ্দেশত কোৱা হয় এটা ভুল আছে, তেন্তে এটা ভুলহে থাকে, যদিও বাক্যটো দুখন ঠাইত সন্দেহজনক যেন লাগে। তেনে ক্ষেত্ৰত প্ৰাৰ্থীয়ে স্পষ্টভাৱে নিয়ম ভঙা অংশটো বাছনি কৰি কেৱল অস্বাভাৱিক শুনা অংশটো এৰি দিব লাগে। প্ৰায়েই এটা ভুল বানানৰ শব্দই চকু টানে, কিন্তু প্ৰকৃত ভুলটো আন ঠাইত থাকে। নিৰ্দেশ মন দি পঢ়া আৰু আটাইতকৈ নিশ্চিত নিয়মত ভৰসা কৰাটোৱেই নিৰাপদ উপায়। নথকা দুটা ভুল অনুমান কৰাটো সহজ নম্বৰ হেৰুওৱাৰ সাধাৰণ উপায়।

    দ্ৰুত উত্তৰৰ চুটি পদ্ধতি

    প্ৰথমে আন যিকোনো কথাতকৈ verb ক subject ৰ সৈতে যাচক। তাৰ পিছত স্থিৰ preposition ৰ যোৰ বিচাৰক, কাৰণ ভুল preposition লুকুৱাটো আটাইতকৈ সহজ। তাৰ পিছত a, an আৰু the article স্কেন কৰি ধ্বনিৰ নিয়মৰ সৈতে যাচক। তাৰ পিছত প্ৰতিটো clause ৰ tense পৰীক্ষা কৰি সময়বোৰ মিলে নে নাই চাওক। শেষত প্ৰতিটো দীঘল শব্দৰ বানান যাচক। এই নিৰ্দিষ্ট ক্ৰমে এটা বিভ্ৰান্তিকৰ বাক্যক এটা চুটি চেকলিষ্টলৈ সলনি কৰে আৰু পৰীক্ষা হলে বহুমূলীয়া সময় ৰাহি কৰে।

    এৰাই চলিবলগীয়া সাধাৰণ ভুল

    • বাক্যটো শুদ্ধ হোৱাৰ পিছতো ভুল বিচাৰি উলিওৱা।
    • প্ৰকৃত ভুল ব্যাকৰণৰ হোৱাৰ পিছতো বানান শুধৰোৱা।
    • ভুল preposition স্বাভাৱিক শুনা বাবে এৰি দিয়া।
    • প্ৰকৃত subject আওকাণ কৰি ওচৰৰ noun ৰ সৈতে verb মিলোৱা।
    • আগৰ পৰাই সামঞ্জস্যপূৰ্ণ বাক্যৰ tense সলোৱা।

    এই বিষয়ৰ প্ৰস্তুতি কেনেকৈ কৰিব

    1. সাধাৰণ বানান নিয়ম আৰু সিহঁত ভঙা শব্দবোৰ শিকক।
    2. যিবোৰ শব্দ কেতিয়াবা ভুল লিখিছে সেইবোৰৰ এখন তালিকা বনাই সাপ্তাহিকভাৱে পুনৰালোচনা কৰক।
    3. এটা নিৰ্দিষ্ট নিয়ম-ক্ৰমত ভুল বিচাৰি উলিওৱাৰ অভ্যাস কৰক।
    4. প্ৰতিদিনে ভাল ইংৰাজীৰ এপৃষ্ঠা পঢ়ি শুদ্ধ কি সেয়া বুজাৰ অনুভূতি গঢ়ক।
    5. আগৰ বছৰৰ প্ৰশ্নপত্ৰ সমাধান কৰি বাৰে বাৰে অহা সাঁচবোৰ টোকা কৰক।

    সাধু সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

    Error spotting আৰু spelling correction একেই নে?

    হুবহু একে নহয়। Spelling correction এ কেৱল ভুল বানানৰ শব্দ লৈ ব্যস্ত থাকে, আনহাতে error spotting ত agreement, tense, preposition বা article ৰ দৰে যিকোনো ব্যাকৰণৰ নিয়ম থাকিব পাৰে। বহু প্ৰশ্নপত্ৰই দুয়োটা একে শিৰোনামাৰ তলত মিলাই দিয়ে, সেয়ে দুয়োটা একেলগে প্ৰস্তুতি কৰিব লাগে।

    এটা ভুল আছে বুলি কেনেকৈ জানিম?

    নিৰ্দেশেই এইটো নিৰ্ণয় কৰে। যদি ই কয় এটা ভুল আছে, তেন্তে স্পষ্টভাৱে এটা নিৰ্দিষ্ট নিয়ম ভঙা অংশটো বাছনি কৰক আৰু দ্বিতীয় ভুল অনুমান নকৰিব। এটা বাক্য দুখন ঠাইত অস্বাভাৱিক শুনা যাব পাৰে, কিন্তু এটাহে প্ৰকৃততে ব্যাকৰণ ভাঙে।

    ভুল preposition কিয় শুদ্ধ যেন লাগে?

    কাৰণ দৈনন্দিন কথা-বতৰাত আমি বহু ভুল যোৰ শুনি অভ্যস্ত হৈ পৰোঁ। নিৰাপদ উপায় হ'ল depend on, listen to আৰু good at ৰ দৰে স্থিৰ যোৰবোৰ মুখস্থ কৰা আৰু প্ৰতিটো preposition সেই তালিকাৰ সৈতে যাচা।

    কোনটো বানান নিয়ম আটাইতকৈ বেছি সোধা হয়?

    i before e ৰ নিয়ম আৰু শেষৰ ব্যঞ্জনধ্বনি দুবাৰ লিখাৰ নিয়ম আটাইতকৈ সঘন। y ৰ i লৈ সলনি হোৱা নিয়ম আৰু silent e ৰ নিয়মো নিয়মীয়াকৈ আহে, সেয়ে চাৰিটো উদাহৰণৰ সৈতে শিকা উচিত।

    বহল পঢ়াই নিয়ম শিকা সলনি কৰিব পাৰিব নে?

    পঢ়াই শুদ্ধ কি সেয়া বুজাৰ অনুভূতি গঢ়ি বহু সহায় কৰে, কিন্তু এনে পৰীক্ষাত য'ত এটা আখৰেও গুৰুত্ব ৰাখে তাত ই নিয়মৰ সলনি হ'ব নোৱাৰে। শ্ৰেষ্ঠ ফল নিয়মৰ স্পষ্ট জ্ঞানৰ সৈতে পঢ়া মিলাই ল'লে পোৱা যায়।
