    Statistics

    Statistics is the branch of mathematics that collects numbers and describes them with a few summary values. In the ADRE Grade III paper the questions ask for the mean, the median, the mode or the range of a small set of numbers, and each of these has a short and reliable method. The chapter is one of the safest in the paper for a candidate who keeps the four ideas apart.

    The mean, also called the arithmetic average, is the total of the values divided by the number of values. It is the value that every item would have if the total were shared equally. The mean is the most used of the four measures and the only one that uses every value in the set.

    The median is the middle value when the numbers are arranged in order. If the number of values is odd, the median is the single middle value; if it is even, the median is the average of the two middle values. The median is not affected by one very large or very small value, which is why it is preferred to the mean when the data contain an outlier.

    The mode is the value that occurs most often. A set may have one mode, more than one mode, or no mode at all if every value occurs the same number of times. The mode is the only one of the four measures that can be used for data that are not numbers, such as the colours of a set of objects.

    The range is the difference between the largest and the smallest value, and it measures how spread out the data are. A small range means the values are close together and a large range means they are widely spread. The range is quick to find and is often asked along with the mean to describe a set completely.

    A weighted average is used when the items do not all carry the same importance. If one group of items has an average of one value and another group has an average of another, the average of the whole is found by multiplying each average by the number of items in its group, adding the products, and dividing by the total number of items. This is the same as the mean of the whole set taken at once.

    The mean of the first n natural numbers has a neat formula: it is n plus one, divided by two. The median of the first n natural numbers is the same value, because the numbers are equally spaced. These two results save a long addition when the question asks about the first ten or the first hundred natural numbers.

    The most common mistakes are these. Candidates forget to arrange the numbers in order before taking the median; they divide by the wrong count; they confuse the mean with the median; and they take the range as the sum instead of the difference. Each is avoided by writing the four definitions on the answer sheet and checking which one the question has asked for.

    A useful check is that the mean must lie between the smallest and the largest value. If a computed mean falls outside that interval, the arithmetic has gone wrong. Similarly, the median must be one of the values or lie between the two middle values, and the range can never be negative.

    For revision, keep the four definitions, the formula for the weighted average, and the two results for the first n natural numbers. These cover every statistics question that the examination sets. The chapter needs no long calculation, and the marks are among the easiest in the paper once the four ideas are kept separate in the mind.

    The mean is affected by every value in the set, so one very large value pulls it upwards. The median is affected only by the position of the values in order, so it stays close to the middle even when one value is extreme. This is the reason a question about house prices or incomes usually asks for the median.

    A frequency table gives each value together with the number of times it occurs. The mean of such a table is found by multiplying each value by its frequency, adding the products, and dividing by the total frequency. The mode is simply the value with the highest frequency, and the median is found by locating the middle position in the cumulative frequency.

    The mean of a set of numbers can be changed in a predictable way. If every value is increased by the same amount, the mean increases by that amount, and if every value is multiplied by the same number, the mean is multiplied by that number. These rules answer questions that ask what happens to the average when every item changes in the same way.

    When the mean of one group and the mean of another group are given, the mean of the two groups taken together is a weighted average, not the simple average of the two means. The group with more items pulls the combined mean towards its own value. This is the single most common trap in the statistics questions of the paper.

    For a final check, remember that the mean, the median and the mode are all measures of the centre of the data, while the range is a measure of the spread. A question that describes the data with a single number is usually asking for one of the three centres, and a question about how scattered the data are is asking for the range.

    To find the mean, add all the values first and then divide the sum by the number of values. To find the median, arrange the values from the smallest to the largest and take the middle one; when the number of values is even, take the mean of the two middle values. Keeping this order in mind reduces mistakes in the questions.

    To find the mode, look for the value that occurs the greatest number of times. If one value repeats more than all the others, that is the mode. If two values occur the same number of times, the data can have two modes, and if no value repeats the data may have no clear mode at all.

    The range is the largest value in the data minus the smallest value. It is a simple measure of how spread out the data are. A large range means the values are far apart, and a small range means they are close together. The mean gives the centre and the range gives the spread, and together they describe the data well.

    Writing the data in order is the most important habit in statistics. Without arranging them first, the median is very likely to be wrong, because the middle position is misjudged. So the first step is always to write the values from the smallest to the largest.

    • Mean = sum of the values / number of values.
    • Median is the middle value after arranging the numbers in order.
    • Mode is the value that occurs most often.
    • Range = largest value - smallest value.
    • Weighted average = total of (average x number of items) / total items.
    • Mean of the first n natural numbers = (n + 1) / 2.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I find the median of an even number of values?

    Arrange the values in order and take the average of the two middle values.

    What is the mode of a set of numbers?

    The mode is the value that appears most frequently in the set. A set may have one mode, several, or none.

    Why is the mean of the first n natural numbers (n + 1) / 2?

    The sum of the first n natural numbers is n(n + 1) / 2, and dividing by n gives (n + 1) / 2.

    মাধ্য উলিয়াবলৈ প্ৰথমে আটাইকেইটা মান যোগ কৰি যোগফলটো মানৰ সংখ্যাৰে হৰণ কৰা হয়। মধ্যক উলিয়াবলৈ মানবোৰ সৰুৰ পৰা ডাঙৰলৈ সজাই মাজৰ মানটো লোৱা হয়; মানৰ সংখ্যা যুগ্ম হ'লে মাজৰ দুটা মানৰ মাধ্য লোৱা হয়। এই ক্ৰমটো মনত ৰাখিলে প্ৰশ্নত ভুল কম হয়।

    বহুলক উলিয়াবলৈ কোনটো মান আটাইতকৈ বেছিবাৰ আহিছে সেয়া চাব লাগে। তথ্যত এটা মান আনবোৰতকৈ বেছি পুনৰাবৃত্তি হ'লে সেয়াই বহুলক। যদি দুটা মান একেই সংখ্যাবাৰ আহে, তেন্তে তথ্যটোৰ দুটা বহুলক থাকিব পাৰে, বা কোনো এটা স্পষ্ট বহুলক নাথাকিবও পাৰে।

    পৰিসৰ হৈছে তথ্যৰ আটাইতকৈ ডাঙৰ মানৰ পৰা আটাইতকৈ সৰু মান বিয়োগ কৰা ফল। ই তথ্য কিমান বিয়পি আছে তাৰ এটা সৰল জোখ। পৰিসৰ ডাঙৰ হ'লে মানবোৰ বেছি আঁতৰ-আঁতৰত, আৰু পৰিসৰ সৰু হ'লে মানবোৰ ওচৰা-উচৰি। মাধ্যই কেন্দ্ৰ দিয়ে, পৰিসৰে বিস্তাৰ দিয়ে, আৰু দুয়োটাই একেলগে তথ্যৰ এটা ভাল ছবি দিয়ে।

    প্ৰশ্নত থকা তথ্যবোৰ সজাই লিখাটোৱেই পৰিসংখ্যাৰ আটাইতকৈ প্ৰয়োজনীয় অভ্যাস। সজাই নিলিখিলে মধ্যক উলিওৱাত ভুল হোৱাৰ সম্ভাৱনা বেছি, কাৰণ মাজৰ অৱস্থানটো ভুল হৈ যায়। গতিকে প্ৰথম কামটো হ'ল তথ্যখিনি সৰুৰ পৰা ডাঙৰলৈ সজাই লিখা।

    • মাধ্য = মানবোৰৰ যোগফল / মানৰ সংখ্যা।
    • মধ্যক হৈছে সংখ্যাবোৰ ক্ৰমত সজাই ল'লে মাজৰ মান।
    • বহুলক হৈছে সৰ্বাধিকবাৰ অহা মান।
    • পৰিসৰ = সৰ্বোচ্চ মান - সৰ্বনিম্ন মান।
    • ভাৰিত গড় = (গড় x বস্তুৰ সংখ্যা) ৰ মুঠ / মুঠ বস্তু।
    • প্ৰথম n স্বাভাৱিক সংখ্যাৰ মাধ্য = (n + 1) / 2।

    সততে সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

    যুগ্ম সংখ্যক মানৰ মধ্যক কেনেকৈ উলিয়াওঁ?

    মানবোৰ ক্ৰমত সজাই মাজৰ দুটা মানৰ গড় লওক।

    সংখ্যা এটা সমষ্টিৰ বহুলক কি?

    বহুলক হৈছে সমষ্টিত সৰ্বাধিকবাৰ দেখা দিয়া মান। এটা সমষ্টিৰ এটা, একাধিক, বা একোটো বহুলক নাথাকিবও পাৰে।

    প্ৰথম n স্বাভাৱিক সংখ্যাৰ মাধ্য কিয় (n + 1) / 2?

    প্ৰথম n স্বাভাৱিক সংখ্যাৰ যোগফল n(n + 1) / 2, আৰু n ৰে হৰণ কৰিলে (n + 1) / 2 পোৱা যায়।
