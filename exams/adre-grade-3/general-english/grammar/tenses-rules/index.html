    Tenses

    Tense is the form of the verb that shows the time of an action and also whether the action is complete, going on or repeated. In the ADRE Grade III paper tense is tested in fill in the blanks, in error spotting and in sentence correction, and it is tested more than any other single verb topic. The difficulty is not that the rules are hard but that the same idea can be expressed by more than one form and the choice depends on the whole sentence. A candidate who learns the three times and the four aspects, and who reads the whole sentence before choosing a form, will find this chapter reliable.

    There are three times, present, past and future, and four aspects, simple, continuous, perfect and perfect continuous, giving twelve tense forms in all. The simple present is used for a habit, a general truth or a fixed arrangement. We write he goes to school every day for a habit, the sun rises in the east for a general truth, and the train leaves at six for a fixed arrangement. The simple present is also used with stative verbs, which describe a state rather than an action, so that we say I know the answer and not I am knowing the answer.

    The present continuous is formed with am, is or are and the ing form, and it is used for an action happening now, an action in progress around now, or a fixed plan in the near future. We write she is reading a book, he is working in Delhi these days, and we are leaving tomorrow. The present perfect is formed with has or have and the past participle, and it is used for an action completed at an unstated time, for an experience, and for an action that began in the past and continues. We write I have finished my work, she has visited Delhi, and he has lived here for ten years.

    The present perfect continuous is formed with has been or have been and the ing form, and it is used for an action that began in the past and is still going on. We write he has been working here since 2015 and it has been raining for two hours. This is the tense that pairs with since and for, and it is the correct form for a sentence such as she has been teaching in this school for five years. A very common error is to use the present continuous in this place, writing she is teaching here for five years, which is wrong because a length of time reaching to the present needs the perfect continuous.

    The simple past is used for an action completed at a definite time in the past. We write he came yesterday, she wrote a letter last night, and the train left at five. The past continuous is used for an action that was going on when another action happened, and the two are joined by when or while. We write he was reading when I called him, and while she was cooking the bell rang. The past perfect is used for the earlier of two past actions, and it is formed with had and the past participle. We write the train had left before I reached the station, and he told me that he had finished the work.

    The past perfect continuous is formed with had been and the ing form, and it is used for an action that had been going on for some time before another past action. We write he had been waiting for an hour when the bus arrived. In reported speech the past perfect often stands for an earlier past, so that he said, I am ill becomes he said that he was ill, and he said, I finished becomes he said that he had finished. The rule of shifting the tense back one step in reported speech is applied to almost every narration question.

    The simple future is formed with shall or will and the base form, and it is used for a prediction, a promise or a decision made at the moment of speaking. We write it will rain tomorrow, I will help you, and I shall return soon. The future continuous is formed with will be and the ing form, as in he will be reading at that time. The future perfect is formed with will have and the past participle, as in he will have finished the work by tomorrow. The future perfect continuous is formed with will have been and the ing form, as in by next June he will have been working here for ten years.

    A few tense rules are asked so often that they deserve to be named. The present simple is used after if in a condition about the future, so that we write if it rains we shall stay at home and not if it will rain. The present perfect is not used with a definite past time; we write he finished the work yesterday and not he has finished the work yesterday. The past perfect is used with before and after to show the earlier action clearly. And after when in a time clause the present or the past simple is used rather than a future form.

    The sequence of tenses must be kept consistent. When the main verb is in the past, the verb in the dependent clause is normally in the past as well, so that we write he said that he was tired and not he said that he is tired. An exception is made for a universal truth, which stays in the present, so that we write the teacher said that the earth revolves round the sun. A second exception is made for a habitual fact, as in he said that he takes tea in the morning.

    Stative verbs are not used in the continuous form, and this rule explains many errors. Verbs of thinking such as know, understand, believe and remember, verbs of feeling such as like, love, hate and want, verbs of possession such as have, own and belong, and verbs of the senses such as see, hear and taste are stative. We write I understand your problem and not I am understanding your problem, and we write she has two cars and not she is having two cars. When such a verb is used in an active sense, the continuous form becomes possible, as in he is seeing a doctor meaning he is consulting one.

    The present perfect continuous and the present continuous are often confused, and the deciding clue is the time expression. If the sentence contains since or for with a length of time reaching to the present, the perfect continuous is needed, as in he has been reading for two hours. If the sentence contains a time expression that points to now, such as now, at present or at this moment, the present continuous is needed, as in he is reading now. Reading the time expression before choosing the form settles most of these questions.

    Two more rules complete the picture. The present perfect is used with just, already, yet, ever and never, so that we write he has just left and have you ever been to Delhi. The past simple is used with ago, last, yesterday and in a stated year, so that we write he left two hours ago and she came last week. A sentence that combines has with yesterday, or left with since, is wrong for this reason alone, and such sentences are common in the error-spotting section.

    To revise, draw a table of the twelve forms with one example sentence each, and mark beside each form the time expressions that go with it. Then take ten sentences a day from a newspaper, find the verb, and name the tense. Write down every sentence in which the tense could be changed and the meaning would change. With this routine for a week, the tense questions in the paper become a matter of reading the time expression and applying the matching form rather than of guessing.

    • Simple present: habit, general truth, fixed arrangement and stative verbs.
    • Present continuous: an action now or a fixed plan in the near future.
    • Present perfect: a finished action at an unstated time, or one continuing to now.
    • Present perfect continuous: since or for with a length of time reaching to the present.
    • Simple past: a finished action at a definite past time.
    • Past perfect: the earlier of two past actions, joined by before or after.
    • The present simple is used after if in a future condition.
    • Use just, already, yet, ever and never with the present perfect, and ago, last and yesterday with the past simple.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why is I am knowing the answer wrong?

    Know is a stative verb and describes a state rather than an action, so it is not used in the continuous form. The correct sentence is I know the answer.

    Why can we not write he has finished the work yesterday?

    The present perfect is not used with a definite past time. With yesterday the past simple is needed, so the correct sentence is he finished the work yesterday.

    What tense is used after if in a future condition?

    The present simple is used after if, so we write if it rains we shall stay at home, and not if it will rain.

    When is the present perfect continuous needed?

    When an action began in the past and is still going on, especially with since or for, as in he has been working here since 2015.

    বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণ-চলিত has been বা have been আৰু ing ৰূপেৰে গঠিত হয়, আৰু অতীতত আৰম্ভ হৈ এতিয়াও চলি থকা কামৰ বাবে ব্যৱহাৰ হয়। আমি he has been working here since 2015 আৰু it has been raining for two hours লিখোঁ। এইটোৱেই সেই কাল যি since আৰু for ৰ সৈতে যায়, আৰু she has been teaching in this school for five years বাক্যৰ বাবে শুদ্ধ ৰূপ। এটা বৰ সাধাৰণ ভুল হৈছে এই ঠাইত বৰ্তমান চলিত ব্যৱহাৰ কৰি she is teaching here for five years লিখা, যি ভুল কাৰণ বৰ্তমানলৈকে পোৱা সময়ৰ দৈৰ্ঘ্যৰ বাবে পূৰ্ণ-চলিত লাগে।

    সৰল অতীত অতীতৰ নিৰ্দিষ্ট সময়ত সম্পূৰ্ণ হোৱা কামৰ বাবে ব্যৱহাৰ হয়। আমি he came yesterday, she wrote a letter last night, আৰু the train left at five লিখোঁ। অতীত চলিত তেতিয়া চলি থকা কামৰ বাবে ব্যৱহাৰ হয় যেতিয়া আন এটা কাম ঘটে, আৰু দুয়োটাক when বা while য়ে জোড়ে। আমি he was reading when I called him, আৰু while she was cooking the bell rang লিখোঁ। অতীত পূৰ্ণ দুটা অতীত কামৰ আগৰটোৰ বাবে ব্যৱহাৰ হয়, আৰু had আৰু past participle ৰে গঠিত হয়। আমি the train had left before I reached the station, আৰু he told me that he had finished the work লিখোঁ।

    অতীত পূৰ্ণ-চলিত had been আৰু ing ৰূপেৰে গঠিত হয়, আৰু আন এটা অতীত কামৰ আগতে কিছু সময় ধৰি চলি থকা কামৰ বাবে ব্যৱহাৰ হয়। আমি he had been waiting for an hour when the bus arrived লিখোঁ। Reported speech ত অতীত পূৰ্ণয়ে প্ৰায়ে আগৰ অতীত বুজায়, গতিকে he said, I am ill ৰ পৰা he said that he was ill হয়, আৰু he said, I finished ৰ পৰা he said that he had finished হয়। Reported speech ত কাল এখোজ পিছুৱাই নিয়াৰ নিয়ম প্ৰায় প্ৰতিটো narration প্ৰশ্নত প্ৰয়োগ হয়।

    সৰল ভৱিষ্যত shall বা will আৰু base ৰূপেৰে গঠিত হয়, আৰু ভৱিষ্যদ্বাণী, প্ৰতিশ্ৰুতি বা কোৱাৰ মুহূৰ্তত লোৱা সিদ্ধান্তৰ বাবে ব্যৱহাৰ হয়। আমি it will rain tomorrow, I will help you, আৰু I shall return soon লিখোঁ। ভৱিষ্যত চলিত will be আৰু ing ৰূপেৰে, যেনে he will be reading at that time। ভৱিষ্যত পূৰ্ণ will have আৰু past participle ৰে, যেনে he will have finished the work by tomorrow। ভৱিষ্যত পূৰ্ণ-চলিত will have been আৰু ing ৰূপেৰে, যেনে by next June he will have been working here for ten years।

    কেইটামান কাল-নিয়ম ইমান বাৰে সোধা হয় যে ইহঁতৰ নাম লোৱাৰ যোগ্য। ভৱিষ্যতৰ চৰ্তত if ৰ পিছত সৰল বৰ্তমান ব্যৱহাৰ হয়, গতিকে if it rains we shall stay at home লিখোঁ, if it will rain নহয়। নিৰ্দিষ্ট অতীত কালৰ সৈতে বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণ ব্যৱহাৰ নহয়; আমি he finished the work yesterday লিখোঁ, he has finished the work yesterday নহয়। আগৰ কাম স্পষ্টকৈ দেখুৱাবলৈ before আৰু after ৰ সৈতে অতীত পূৰ্ণ ব্যৱহাৰ হয়। আৰু সময়ৰ উপবাক্যত when ৰ পিছত ভৱিষ্যত ৰূপৰ সলনি সৰল বৰ্তমান বা অতীত ব্যৱহাৰ হয়।

    কালৰ ক্ৰম (sequence of tenses) সামঞ্জস্যপূৰ্ণ ৰাখিব লাগে। মূল ক্ৰিয়া অতীতত হ'লে, আশ্ৰিত উপবাক্যৰ ক্ৰিয়াও সাধাৰণতে অতীতত থাকে, গতিকে he said that he was tired লিখোঁ, he said that he is tired নহয়। চিৰন্তন সত্যৰ বাবে এটা ব্যতিক্ৰম, যি বৰ্তমানতে থাকে, গতিকে the teacher said that the earth revolves round the sun লিখোঁ। অভ্যাসগত সত্যৰ বাবেও দ্বিতীয় এটা ব্যতিক্ৰম, যেনে he said that he takes tea in the morning।

    Stative verb চলিত ৰূপত ব্যৱহাৰ নহয়, আৰু এই নিয়মে বহু ভুল ব্যাখ্যা কৰে। ভাৱনাৰ ক্ৰিয়া যেনে know, understand, believe, remember; অনুভৱৰ ক্ৰিয়া যেনে like, love, hate, want; মালিকীৰ ক্ৰিয়া যেনে have, own, belong; আৰু ইন্দ্ৰিয়ৰ ক্ৰিয়া যেনে see, hear, taste — এইবোৰ stative। আমি I understand your problem লিখোঁ, I am understanding your problem নহয়; আৰু she has two cars লিখোঁ, she is having two cars নহয়। এনে ক্ৰিয়া সক্ৰিয় অৰ্থত ব্যৱহাৰ হ'লে চলিত ৰূপ সম্ভৱ হ'ব পাৰে, যেনে he is seeing a doctor মানে তেওঁ এজন ডাক্তৰক লগ কৰি আছে।

    বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণ-চলিত আৰু বৰ্তমান চলিত প্ৰায়ে মিলাই পেলোৱা হয়, আৰু নিৰ্ণায়ক সূত্ৰটো হ'ল সময়ৰ প্ৰকাশ। বাক্যত বৰ্তমানলৈকে পোৱা সময়ৰ দৈৰ্ঘ্যৰ সৈতে since বা for থাকিলে পূৰ্ণ-চলিত লাগে, যেনে he has been reading for two hours। বাক্যত এতিয়াৰ দিশ দেখুওৱা সময়ৰ প্ৰকাশ, যেনে now, at present বা at this moment থাকিলে বৰ্তমান চলিত লাগে, যেনে he is reading now। ৰূপ বাছনি কৰাৰ আগতে সময়ৰ প্ৰকাশ পঢ়িলে এই প্ৰশ্নবোৰৰ বেছিভাগ মিটমাট হয়।

    আৰু দুটা নিয়মে ছবিখন সম্পূৰ্ণ কৰে। বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণ just, already, yet, ever আৰু never ৰ সৈতে ব্যৱহাৰ হয়, গতিকে he has just left আৰু have you ever been to Delhi লিখোঁ। সৰল অতীত ago, last, yesterday আৰু উল্লেখ কৰা বছৰৰ সৈতে ব্যৱহাৰ হয়, গতিকে he left two hours ago আৰু she came last week লিখোঁ। has ৰ সৈতে yesterday, বা left ৰ সৈতে since ৰ সংযোগ কৰা বাক্য এই কাৰণতে ভুল, আৰু এনে বাক্য ভুল নিৰ্ণয় অংশত সাধাৰণ।

    পুনৰালোচনাৰ বাবে বাৰটা ৰূপৰ এখন তালিকা প্ৰতিটোৰ এটা উদাহৰণ বাক্যৰ সৈতে আঁকক, আৰু প্ৰতিটো ৰূপৰ কাষত তাৰ লগত যোৱা সময়ৰ প্ৰকাশ লিখক। তাৰপিছত প্ৰতিদিনে বাতৰি কাকতৰ পৰা দহটা বাক্য লৈ ক্ৰিয়া বিচাৰি কালটোৰ নাম কওক। যিবোৰ বাক্যত কাল সলনি কৰিলে অৰ্থ সলনি হয়, সেইবোৰ লিখি ৰাখক। এই ৰুটিন এসপ্তাহ চলালে পত্ৰৰ কাল-প্ৰশ্নবোৰ অনুমান কৰাৰ সলনি সময়ৰ প্ৰকাশ পঢ়ি মিলোৱা ৰূপ প্ৰয়োগ কৰাৰ কাম হৈ পৰে।

    • সৰল বৰ্তমান: অভ্যাস, সাধাৰণ সত্য, নিৰ্ধাৰিত ব্যৱস্থা আৰু stative verb।
    • বৰ্তমান চলিত: এতিয়া হোৱা কাম বা ওচৰ ভৱিষ্যতৰ নিৰ্ধাৰিত পৰিকল্পনা।
    • বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণ: অনিৰ্দিষ্ট সময়ত সম্পূৰ্ণ কাম, বা এতিয়ালৈকে চলি থকা কাম।
    • বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণ-চলিত: বৰ্তমানলৈকে পোৱা সময়ৰ দৈৰ্ঘ্যৰ সৈতে since বা for।
    • সৰল অতীত: অতীতৰ নিৰ্দিষ্ট সময়ত সম্পূৰ্ণ হোৱা কাম।
    • অতীত পূৰ্ণ: দুটা অতীত কামৰ আগৰটো, before বা after য়ে জোড়ে।
    • ভৱিষ্যতৰ চৰ্তত if ৰ পিছত সৰল বৰ্তমান ব্যৱহাৰ হয়।
    • Just, already, yet, ever, never বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণৰ সৈতে, আৰু ago, last, yesterday সৰল অতীতৰ সৈতে ব্যৱহাৰ কৰক।
