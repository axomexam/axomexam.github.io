    Spotting Errors

    Spotting errors is the chapter in which a sentence is given and the candidate must find the part that is wrong. The sentence is usually divided into three or four parts, and one of the parts contains a mistake of grammar, of word choice or of idiom. In the ADRE Grade III paper this chapter carries a good number of marks, and it rewards a candidate who reads with a rule in mind rather than with a feeling. Almost every error belongs to one of a small number of families, and once the families are known the errors become easy to see.

    The largest family of errors is subject-verb agreement. The verb must match the true subject and not the noun nearest to it, so that the sentence the quality of the mangoes were good is wrong because the true subject is quality. A phrase beginning with with, along with, as well as or together with does not change the number of the subject, and a relative pronoun takes the number of its antecedent. Checking the verb against the true subject is the first thing to do in every sentence.

    The second family is the wrong form of the verb, including the wrong tense and the wrong participle. A sentence that contains a definite past time such as yesterday needs the simple past and not the present perfect, so that he has gone yesterday is wrong. A sentence that contains since or for with a time reaching the present needs the present perfect continuous. And a sentence that uses the passive needs the past participle and not the base form, so that the letter was write is wrong.

    The third family is the wrong preposition. A verb or an adjective must take the preposition that belongs to it, so that married with is wrong and married to is right, discuss about is wrong and discuss is right, and different than is usually wrong where different from is right. A preposition must also be followed by a noun, an objective pronoun or a gerund, so that between you and I is wrong. Checking every preposition against the word before it finds this family of errors quickly.

    The fourth family is the wrong pronoun, in number, in case or in reference. A pronoun must agree with the noun it stands for, so that the sentence every student must bring their book is wrong in formal writing because every is singular. A pronoun after a preposition takes the objective case, so that between you and I is wrong. And a pronoun must have a clear antecedent, so that a sentence in which it could refer to two different nouns is faulty.

    The fifth family is the wrong article or determiner. A or an is chosen by sound, so that a honest man is wrong and an honest man is right. The is used for something definite, so that a missing the before a superlative or before the name of a river is an error. And a determiner must match its noun in number, so that these kind of books is wrong. Reading each noun with its article or determiner finds this family at once.

    The sixth family is the wrong degree of comparison. The comparative degree compares two and is followed by than, while the superlative compares more than two and takes the. So the sentence he is more better than his brother is wrong because two comparatives are used, and the sentence she is the most tallest girl is wrong for the same reason. A double comparative and a double superlative are both common errors, and both are easy to see once the form of the adjective is checked.

    The seventh family is the wrong word, either a confusion between two words that look alike or a use of the wrong part of speech. The sentence he gave me a good advise is wrong because advise is a verb and advice is the noun. The sentence she sings very good is wrong because an adverb is needed. And the sentence I am staying here since morning is wrong because the present perfect continuous is needed. Checking the part of speech of each underlined word finds this family.

    The eighth family is redundancy, in which a word repeats a meaning already present. The sentence he returned back is wrong because return already contains the idea of back. The sentence repeat again, discuss about and more better are wrong for the same reason. A sentence that says the reason is because joins two words that do the same work, and a sentence that says the most unique uses an absolute adjective with a comparative form.

    The ninth family is the wrong conjunction, in which the two halves of a pair do not match. Either must be paired with or, neither with nor, both with and, not only with but also, and no sooner with than. A sentence that says neither Ram or Shyam is wrong, and a sentence that says no sooner had he come when it began to rain is wrong because no sooner takes than. Checking each pair of conjunctions is a quick way to find an error.

    The tenth family is the wrong order of words, which is tested both here and in sentence rearrangement. An adverb of frequency goes before the main verb and after the auxiliary, so that he always is late is wrong and he is always late is right. An adjective goes before its noun, and an adjective phrase follows it. And the words only, even and almost must stand before the word they modify, so that a misplaced only changes the meaning of the whole sentence.

    A practical method for the examination is to read the sentence once for the sense, then read it again with the ten families in mind, checking the subject and the verb first, then the tense, then the preposition, then the pronoun, then the article, then the degree, then the word, then the redundancy, then the conjunction and finally the order. Most errors fall into the first four families, so the check need not take long. When two parts look correct, the part that contains an unusual word or an unusual form is usually the faulty one.

    To revise, take ten sentences a day from a newspaper or a grammar book, mark the part that seems faulty, and write beside it the name of the family to which the error belongs. Keep a notebook of the errors met, arranged by family. Because the same families recur year after year, a candidate who learns to name the family of an error can find it in a few seconds, and this chapter then becomes one of the most reliable in the paper.

    A good technique for practice is to take a sentence and check the subject and the verb first, then the tense, then the preposition, and then the pronoun. Checking in this order catches most errors within the first four steps, and the remaining families are usually seen at the end. Checking ten sentences a day in this way and noting the family of each error trains the eye to find the fault in the paper.

    While practising, the most valuable habit is to write the name of the family beside every faulty sentence. Writing them down in this way shows that most errors repeat within a few families, and when the rules of those families are firm, the eye catches the fault by itself when a sentence is read in the paper.

    Continuity is the main thing in preparation. Ten sentences a day, finding the fault in each and writing down the family to which it belongs, is enough. After a month of this, the eye checks the subject-verb agreement, the tense, the preposition and the pronoun by itself when any sentence is read, and the faulty part is caught in a few seconds in the paper. Practised in this way, the chapter becomes one of the highest-scoring in General English.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why is the quality of the mangoes were good wrong?

    The true subject is quality and it is singular, so the verb must be was. The phrase of the mangoes does not change the number of the subject.

    Why is he returned back wrong?

    Because return already contains the sense of back, so adding back repeats the meaning. The correct sentence is he returned.

    What is wrong with more better?

    Better is already the comparative degree of good, so more cannot be added. The correct form is better, or much better.

    How many families of errors should I check?

    Ten: agreement, verb form, preposition, pronoun, article, degree, word choice, redundancy, conjunction and word order. Most errors fall in the first four.

    পৰীক্ষাৰ বাবে এটা ব্যৱহৃত পদ্ধতি হ'ল প্ৰথমে অৰ্থৰ বাবে বাক্যটো এবাৰ পঢ়া, তাৰপিছত দহটা পৰিয়াল মনত ৰাখি আকৌ পঢ়া — প্ৰথমে কৰ্তা আৰু ক্ৰিয়া, তাৰপিছত কাল, তাৰপিছত পূৰ্বসৰ্গ, তাৰপিছত সৰ্বনাম, তাৰপিছত article, তাৰপিছত স্তৰ, তাৰপিছত শব্দ, তাৰপিছত পুনৰুক্তি, তাৰপিছত সংযোগ আৰু শেষত ক্ৰম। বেছিভাগ ভুল প্ৰথম চাৰিটা পৰিয়ালত পৰে, গতিকে পৰীক্ষাটো বেছি সময় নলয়। দুটা অংশ শুদ্ধ দেখা গ'লে, অস্বাভাৱিক শব্দ বা অস্বাভাৱিক ৰূপ থকা অংশটোৱেই সাধাৰণতে ত্ৰুটিপূৰ্ণ।

    পুনৰালোচনাৰ বাবে বাতৰি কাকত বা ব্যাকৰণ কিতাপৰ পৰা প্ৰতিদিনে দহটা বাক্য লৈ ত্ৰুটিপূৰ্ণ দেখা অংশটো চিহ্নিত কৰক, আৰু কাষত ভুলটোৱে ক'ত পৰে সেই পৰিয়ালৰ নাম লিখক। লগ পোৱা ভুলবোৰ পৰিয়াল অনুসৰি সজাই এখন টোকাবহী ৰাখক। একেই পৰিয়ালবোৰ বছৰে বছৰে পুনৰাবৃত্তি হয়, গতিকে যি প্ৰাৰ্থীয়ে ভুল এটাৰ পৰিয়ালৰ নাম ক'ব শিকে তেওঁ কেইচেকেণ্ডত বিচাৰি পায়, আৰু তেতিয়া এই অধ্যায় পত্ৰৰ আটাইতকৈ নিৰ্ভৰযোগ্য অধ্যায়বোৰৰ এটা হৈ পৰে।

    অনুশীলনৰ বাবে এটা ভাল কৌশল হ'ল এটা বাক্য লৈ প্ৰথমে কৰ্তা-ক্ৰিয়া মিলাই চোৱা, তাৰপিছত কাল, তাৰপিছত পূৰ্বসৰ্গ, আৰু তাৰপিছত সৰ্বনাম পৰীক্ষা কৰা। এই ক্ৰমে চালে বেছিভাগ ভুল চাৰি স্তৰতে ধৰা পৰে, আৰু পূৰ্বে উল্লেখ কৰা দহটা পৰিয়ালৰ বাকীবোৰ প্ৰায়ে শেষতহে দেখা যায়। প্ৰতিদিনে দহটা বাক্য এনেদৰে পৰীক্ষা কৰি ভুলটোৱে ক'ত পৰে টুকি ৰাখিলে, পৰীক্ষাত চকুৱে নিজেই ভুল বিচাৰি লয়।

    অভ্যাস কৰোঁতে প্ৰতিটো ভুল থকা বাক্যৰ কাষত ভুলটোৱে ক'ত পৰে সেই পৰিয়ালৰ নাম লিখি ৰখাটোৱেই আটাইতকৈ মূল্যৱান। এনেদৰে লিখি গ'লে দেখা যাবে যে বেছিভাগ ভুল মাত্ৰ কেইটামান পৰিয়ালতে পুনৰাবৃত্তি হয়, আৰু সেই পৰিয়ালবোৰৰ নিয়ম টান হ'লে পৰীক্ষাত বাক্য চাওঁতে চকুৱে ভুলটো নিজেই ধৰি লয়।

    প্ৰস্তুতিত ধাৰাবাহিকতা ৰখাটোৱেই মূল কথা। দিনে দহটা বাক্য লৈ, প্ৰতিটোত ভুল বিচাৰি, ভুলটোৱে কোন পৰিয়ালৰ তাক লিখি ৰখাটোৱেই যথেষ্ট। এনেদৰে এমাহ চলালে যিকোনো বাক্য চাওঁতে চকুৱে কৰ্তা-ক্ৰিয়া মিল, কাল, পূৰ্বসৰ্গ আৰু সৰ্বনাম আপোনা-আপুনি পৰীক্ষা কৰি লয়, আৰু পৰীক্ষাত ভুল থকা অংশটো কেইচেকেণ্ডতে ধৰা পৰে। এই অধ্যায় এইদৰে অনুশীলন কৰিলে সাধাৰণ ইংৰাজীত আটাইতকৈ বেছি নম্বৰ দিয়া অধ্যায়বোৰৰ এটা হৈ পৰে।

    প্ৰতিদিনে বাতৰি কাকতৰ পৰা দহটা বাক্য লৈ, প্ৰতিটোত ভুল বিচাৰি, ভুলটোৱে কোন পৰিয়ালৰ তাক লিখি ৰখাটোৱেই এই অধ্যায়ৰ আটাইতকৈ প্ৰভাৱশালী অনুশীলন। এইদৰে এমাহ চলালে ভুলবোৰ চকুত পৰিবলৈ ধৰে, আৰু পৰীক্ষাত দীঘল বাক্য এটাত ভুল থকা অংশটোও কেইচেকেণ্ডতে ধৰা পৰে। এই অভ্যাসে এই অধ্যায়ক সাধাৰণ ইংৰাজীত নিশ্চিত নম্বৰৰ উৎস কৰি তোলে।

    শেষত এটা কথা — এই অধ্যায়ত নম্বৰ ৰখাৰ ৰহস্য কোনো জটিল নিয়মত নাই, আছে মাত্ৰ এটা সৰল অভ্যাসত: প্ৰতিদিনে কেইটামান বাক্য মন দি পঢ়া আৰু ভুলটো ক'ত সেইটো নাম দিয়া। এই অভ্যাস এমাহ চলালে ভুলবোৰ চকুত পৰিবলৈ ধৰে, আৰু পৰীক্ষাত যিকোনো বাক্য এটা চাওঁতে ভুল থকা অংশটো আপোনা-আপুনি ধৰা পৰে। সাধাৰণ ইংৰাজীত আটাইতকৈ বেছি নম্বৰ দিয়া অধ্যায়বোৰৰ এটা হৈ পৰে এইটোৱেই।

    • ক্ৰিয়া প্ৰকৃত কৰ্তাৰ সৈতে মিলাওক, ওচৰৰ বিশেষ্যৰ সৈতে নহয়।
    • নিৰ্দিষ্ট অতীত সময়ত সৰল অতীত লাগে, বৰ্তমান পূৰ্ণ নহয়।
    • প্ৰতিটো ক্ৰিয়া আৰু বিশেষণ তাৰ নিৰ্দিষ্ট পূৰ্বসৰ্গৰ সৈতে শিকক।
    • সৰ্বনামে তাৰ বিশেষ্যৰ সৈতে মিলিব লাগে আৰু পূৰ্বসৰ্গৰ পিছত কৰ্ম কাৰক লয়।

    • দুটা তুলনাত্মক আৰু দুটা অতিশয় ব্যৱহাৰ নকৰক।
