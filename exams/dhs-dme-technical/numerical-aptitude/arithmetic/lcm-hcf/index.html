    Q1. The HCF of two numbers is 12 and their LCM is 336. If one number is 48, the other is
    দুটা সংখ্যাৰ গ.সা.গু. ১২ আৰু ল.সা.গু. ৩৩৬। এটা সংখ্যা ৪৮ হ'লে আনটো কিমান
    (A) 72 ৭২
    (B) 84 ৮৪
    (C) 96 ৯৬
    (D) 108 ১০৮
    Answer: B) 84
    B) ৮৪
    Explanation: For two numbers, HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. So the other number is (12 × 336) ÷ 48 = 4032 ÷ 48 = 84.
    দুটা সংখ্যাৰ ক্ষেত্ৰত গ.সা.গু. × ল.সা.গু. = সংখ্যা দুটাৰ গুণফল। সেয়ে আনটো সংখ্যা = (১২ × ৩৩৬) ÷ ৪৮ = ৪০৩২ ÷ ৪৮ = ৮৪।

    LCM & HCF: study notes

    Read this before you attempt the questions. It is written for the DHS & DME Technical Grade III paper.

    LCM & HCF for the DHS & DME Technical Grade III

    LCM and HCF are the two ideas that tie the whole of arithmetic together, and in the DHS & DME Technical Grade III Mathematics paper they are asked both as direct sums and as the hidden engine inside word problems. The highest common factor, or HCF, is the greatest number that divides each of the given numbers exactly. The least common multiple, or LCM, is the smallest number that each of the given numbers divides exactly. Every question in this chapter is built from one of these two, or from a word problem that has been dressed up so that the HCF or LCM is not named.

    The first skill is prime factorisation. Break every number into a product of powers of primes, for example 24 is 2 cubed times 3 and 36 is 2 squared times 3 squared. The HCF is then the product of the lowest power of each prime that appears in all the numbers, so HCF(24, 36) is 2 squared times 3, that is 12. The LCM is the product of the highest power of each prime that appears in any of the numbers, so LCM(24, 36) is 2 cubed times 3 squared, that is 72. This one method answers both questions at once and never fails, however large the numbers.

    The relation between the two is the most useful single fact in the chapter. For any two numbers, the product of their HCF and their LCM equals the product of the numbers themselves. So if three of the four values are known the fourth can be found at once, and a whole family of questions that give the HCF and LCM and ask for a missing number is solved in one line. A related and very common shape is that the numbers are in a given ratio; if two numbers are in the ratio a to b, their HCF is the common factor and their LCM is that common factor multiplied by a and by b.

    The word problems divide into three shapes, and the whole skill is recognising which one a question has. If the question asks for the least number that leaves the same remainder when divided by several numbers, the answer is the LCM of those numbers plus the common remainder. If it asks for the greatest number that divides several numbers leaving the same remainder in each case, the answer is the HCF of the differences of those numbers. If it asks for the greatest number that divides several numbers leaving given different remainders, first subtract each remainder from its number and then take the HCF. These three shapes cover nearly every application.

    The bells and traffic-light problems are the classic LCM application. If several bells ring at given intervals, they ring together again after a time equal to the LCM of the intervals. The same idea answers questions about runners on a circular track meeting again at the starting point, about soldiers arranged in rows, and about rectangular tiles forming the smallest square, where the side of the square is the LCM of the tile sides. Whenever the words least or together appear in a problem about repeated events, the LCM is almost certainly wanted.

    Questions on fractions and decimals need a slightly different form. The LCM of fractions is the LCM of the numerators divided by the HCF of the denominators, and the HCF of fractions is the HCF of the numerators divided by the LCM of the denominators. For decimals, first write each decimal as a fraction with a power of ten in the denominator, then apply the same rule, and convert the answer back to a decimal. A question may give the numbers directly as fractions or as decimals such as 0.6 and 0.9, so both forms should be practised until the conversion is automatic.

    Some questions ask for the number of pairs of numbers that satisfy a condition. If the HCF is given and the sum of the two numbers is given, write the numbers as the HCF multiplied by two co-prime factors, and count the co-prime pairs that add up to the required total. This is a favourite shape because it tests both the HCF relation and the meaning of co-prime, and it appears again and again in the tougher papers. The number of such pairs is usually small, so it is quick to count once the two numbers have been put in the form HCF times a and HCF times b.

    Co-prime numbers deserve a paragraph of their own. Two numbers are co-prime if their HCF is 1, which means they share no prime factor. The LCM of two co-prime numbers is simply their product, and this saves time in several question types. Two consecutive natural numbers are always co-prime, so their HCF is 1. Two consecutive even numbers are never co-prime, because they share the factor 2. Questions that ask for the HCF or LCM of consecutive numbers are answered by these two facts alone.

    Finally, the chapter rewards a few memorised results. The HCF of a set of numbers never exceeds the smallest number in the set, and the LCM never falls below the largest number. The HCF divides the LCM exactly, because the LCM contains every prime power that any of the numbers contains. And for three numbers the product of the HCF and LCM is not equal to the product of the numbers, so the two-number relation must not be carried over carelessly. A candidate who keeps these limits and relations in mind can often eliminate two or three options at a glance before doing any calculation.

    Practice in this chapter should focus on speed and on recognising the shape of the question. Learn to factorise small numbers on sight, keep the HCF and LCM relation ready, and train yourself to read a word problem and decide within a few seconds whether it wants the LCM, the HCF of differences, or the HCF of numbers after subtracting remainders. Because the numbers in the paper are chosen to be manageable, a clear method beats raw calculation every time, and this chapter then becomes one of the most dependable sources of marks in the mathematics section.

    LCM and HCF

    The chapter on the highest common factor and the lowest common multiple is small in appearance but wide in reach, because almost every question that talks about things meeting again, things being divided equally, or things being filled to the brim is really a question about the HCF or the LCM. In the DHS & DME Technical Grade III paper these two ideas are tested through short, practical problems, and a candidate who can tell at a glance which of the two is wanted will finish the section quickly.

    The highest common factor of two or more numbers is the largest number that divides each of them exactly, leaving no remainder. It is also called the greatest common divisor. The lowest common multiple is the smallest number that each of the given numbers divides exactly. For two numbers the product of the HCF and the LCM is always equal to the product of the numbers themselves, a fact that lets a candidate find one of the two when the other is known.

    The HCF is found by writing each number as a product of prime factors and taking the common factors with the lowest powers. The LCM is found from the same factorisation by taking every factor, common or not, with the highest power. For example, to find the HCF and LCM of twenty-four and thirty-six, write twenty-four as two cubed times three and thirty-six as two squared times three squared; the HCF is two squared times three, which is twelve, and the LCM is two cubed times three squared, which is seventy-two. The division method, in which the numbers are divided by common primes until no common prime remains, is a faster route when the numbers are large.

    Two numbers are co-prime when their highest common factor is one. Co-prime numbers need not themselves be prime; eight and fifteen are co-prime even though neither is prime, because they share no common factor other than one. The HCF of two co-prime numbers is one and their LCM is the product of the two numbers. This fact appears often in questions that ask for the least number which is exactly divisible by two given numbers.

    Word problems decide which of the two ideas to use by the sense of the question. If things are to be divided into equal groups, or the largest tile that can cover a floor exactly, or the greatest number that divides several numbers leaving the same remainder, the HCF is wanted. If things meet again, or bells toll together, or runners on a circular track come together again, or a number must be exactly divisible by several numbers, the LCM is wanted. Reading the question once more before starting the calculation saves the candidate from solving the wrong problem correctly.

    প্ৰতিটো সংখ্যাক মৌলিক উৎপাদকৰ গুণফল হিচাপে লিখি আৰু সৰু ঘাত থকা সাধাৰণ উৎপাদকবোৰ লৈ গ.সা.গু. পোৱা যায়। একে উৎপাদকীকৰণৰ পৰা সাধাৰণ হওক বা নহওক, প্ৰতিটো উৎপাদক আটাইতকৈ বেছি ঘাতত লৈ ল.সা.গু. পোৱা যায়। উদাহৰণস্বৰূপে, চৌবিশ আৰু ছত্ৰিশৰ গ.সা.গু. আৰু ল.সা.গু. উলিয়াবলৈ চৌবিশক দুইৰ তৃতীয় ঘাত তিনিগুণ আৰু ছত্ৰিশক দুইৰ বৰ্গ তিনিভৰ বৰ্গ হিচাপে লিখক; গ.সা.গু. হৈছে দুইৰ বৰ্গ তিনিগুণ, অৰ্থাৎ বাৰ, আৰু ল.সা.গু. হৈছে দুইৰ তৃতীয় ঘাত তিনিভৰ বৰ্গ, অৰ্থাৎ সত্তৰ দুই। সংখ্যাবোৰ ডাঙৰ হ'লে সাধাৰণ মৌলিকেৰে ভাগ কৰি যোৱা ভাগ পদ্ধতিটো দ্ৰুত উপায়।

    দুটা সংখ্যাৰ গৰিষ্ঠ সাধাৰণ উৎপাদক এক হ'লে তেওঁলোক সহ-মৌলিক। সহ-মৌলিক সংখ্যা নিজেই মৌলিক হোৱাৰ প্ৰয়োজন নাই; আঠ আৰু পোন্ধৰ সহ-মৌলিক যদিও দুয়োটাই মৌলিক নহয়, কাৰণ তেওঁলোকে একৰ বাহিৰে কোনো সাধাৰণ উৎপাদক ভাগ নলয়। দুটা সহ-মৌলিক সংখ্যাৰ গ.সা.গু. এক আৰু ল.সা.গু. সংখ্যা দুটাৰ গুণফল। এই তথ্যটো সঘনাই সেই প্ৰশ্নবোৰত দেখা দিয়ে যিবোৰে দুটা প্ৰদত্ত সংখ্যাৰে সম্পূৰ্ণৰূপে বিভাজ্য আটাইতকৈ সৰু সংখ্যা সোধে।

    শব্দৰ সমস্যাই প্ৰশ্নৰ অৰ্থৰ জৰিয়তে দুটা ধাৰণাৰ কোনটো ব্যৱহাৰ কৰিব লাগে সেয়া নিৰ্ধাৰণ কৰে। যদি বস্তুবোৰ সমান গোটত ভগ কৰিব লাগে, বা মজিয়াখন সম্পূৰ্ণৰূপে ঢাকিব পৰা আটাইতকৈ ডাঙৰ টাইল বিচৰা হয়, বা কেইবাটাও সংখ্যাক একে ভাগশেষ এৰি ভাগ কৰা আটাইতকৈ ডাঙৰ সংখ্যা বিচৰা হয়, তেন্তে গ.সা.গু. বিচৰা হয়। যদি বস্তুবোৰ পুনৰ লগ পায়, বা ঘণ্টাবোৰ একেলগে বাজে, বা বৃত্তাকাৰ পথত দৌৰুৱাবোৰ পুনৰ একেলগে আহে, বা এটা সংখ্যা কেইবাটাও সংখ্যাৰে সম্পূৰ্ণৰূপে বিভাজ্য হ'ব লাগে, তেন্তে ল.সা.গু. বিচৰা হয়। গণনা আৰম্ভ কৰাৰ আগতে প্ৰশ্নটো আৰু এবাৰ পঢ়িলে প্ৰাৰ্থীক ভুল সমস্যা সঠিকভাৱে সমাধান কৰাৰ পৰা ৰক্ষা কৰে।

    ভাগশেষৰ সমস্যাৰ নিজস্ব সৰু নিয়ম আছে। a, b আৰু cক প্ৰতিটো ক্ষেত্ৰত একে ভাগশেষ r এৰি ভাগ কৰা আটাইতকৈ ডাঙৰ সংখ্যা উলিয়াবলৈ প্ৰতিটো সংখ্যাৰ পৰা r বিয়োগ কৰি ফলাফলবোৰৰ গ.সা.গু. ল'ব লাগে। a, b আৰু cক একে কিন্তু অজ্ঞাত ভাগশেষ এৰি ভাগ কৰা আটাইতকৈ ডাঙৰ সংখ্যা উলিয়াবলৈ সংখ্যাবোৰৰ মাজৰ পাৰ্থক্যবোৰৰ গ.সা.গু. ল'ব লাগে। a, b আৰু cৰে ভাগ কৰিলে প্ৰতিটো ক্ষেত্ৰত একে ভাগশেষ r এৰি যোৱা আটাইতকৈ সৰু সংখ্যা উলিয়াবলৈ a, b আৰু cৰ ল.সা.গু. উলিয়াই r যোগ কৰিব লাগে।

    প্ৰতিটো ভাগশেষ ভাজকতকৈ একে পৰিমাণে কম হোৱা আটাইতকৈ সৰু সংখ্যা ল.সা.গু. উলিয়াই সেই সাধাৰণ অভাৱটো বিয়োগ কৰি পোৱা যায়। উদাহৰণস্বৰূপে, ছয়, আঠ আৰু বাৰৰে ভাগ কৰিলে প্ৰতিটো ক্ষেত্ৰত পাঁচ ভাগশেষ এৰি যোৱা আটাইতকৈ সৰু সংখ্যা হৈছে ছয়, আঠ আৰু বাৰৰ ল.সা.গু., অৰ্থাৎ চৌবিশ, যোগ পাঁচ, যাৰ ফলত ঊনত্ৰিশ। এক অভাৱৰ একে প্ৰশ্ন হৈছে ল.সা.গু. বিয়োগ এক। প্ৰাৰ্থীয়ে দুয়োটা ৰূপ অনুশীলন কৰা উচিত যেতিয়ালৈকে পাৰ্থক্যটো স্বয়ংক্ৰিয় নহয়।

    ভগ্নাংশৰ ল.সা.গু. হৈছে লৱবোৰৰ ল.সা.গু. হৰবোৰৰ গ.সা.গু.ৰে ভাগ কৰা মান, আৰু ভগ্নাংশৰ গ.সা.গু. হৈছে লৱবোৰৰ গ.সা.গু. হৰবোৰৰ ল.সা.গু.ৰে ভাগ কৰা মান। এই দুটা ফলাফল প্ৰাৰ্থীয়ে আশা কৰাতকৈ সঘনাই পত্ৰত দেখা দিয়ে, আৰু ইয়াক মনত ৰখা যিকোনো লোকৰ বাবে ই সহজ নম্বৰ। এটা পূৰ্ণ সংখ্যাক সঠিক ভগ্নাংশৰ সৈতে মিলালে হৰ এক বুলি ধৰা হয়।

    এই প্ৰশ্নগোটৰ পৰা সমাধান কৰা প্ৰশ্ন

    প্ৰ. দুটা সংখ্যাৰ গ.সা.গু. ১২ আৰু ল.সা.গু. ৩৩৬। এটা সংখ্যা ৪৮ হ'লে আনটো কিমান (A) ৭২ (B) ৮৪ (C) ৯৬ (D) ১০৮ শুদ্ধ উত্তৰ ৮৪। দুটা সংখ্যাৰ ক্ষেত্ৰত গ.সা.গু. × ল.সা.গু. = সংখ্যা দুটাৰ গুণফল। সেয়ে আনটো সংখ্যা = (১২ × ৩৩৬) ÷ ৪৮ = ৪০৩২ ÷ ৪৮ = ৮৪।

    প্ৰ. ১২, ১৫ আৰু ১৮ ৰে ভাগ কৰিলে প্ৰতিটো ক্ষেত্ৰতে ভাগশেষ ৫ ৰখা আটাইতকৈ সৰু সংখ্যাটো কি (A) ১৮৫ (B) ১৭৫ (C) ৩৬৫ (D) ৫৪৫ শুদ্ধ উত্তৰ ১৮৫। ১২, ১৫, ১৮ ৰ ল.সা.গু. ১৮০। প্ৰয়োজনীয় সংখ্যা ১৮০ + ৫ = ১৮৫।

    প্ৰ. ২৪, ৩৬ আৰু ৫৪ ৰ ল.সা.গু. নিৰ্ণয় কৰা। (A) ১০৮ (B) ২১৬ (C) ৪৩২ (D) ৭৭৭৬ শুদ্ধ উত্তৰ ২১৬। ল.সা.গু.(২৪,৩৬,৫৪) = ২১৬।

    প্ৰ. ২৪, ৩৬ আৰু ৫৪ ৰ গ.সা.গু. নিৰ্ণয় কৰা। (A) ১২ (B) ৭ (C) ৬ (D) ৪ শুদ্ধ উত্তৰ ৬। গ.সা.গু.(২৪,৩৬,৫৪) = ৬।
