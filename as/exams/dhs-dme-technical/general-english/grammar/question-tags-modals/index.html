    Question Tags & Modals: study notes

    Read this before you attempt the questions. It is written for the DHS & DME Technical Grade III paper.

    Question tags: the shape of the sentence

    A question tag is the short question added at the end of a statement, as in You are coming, aren't you? The tag turns a statement into a request for confirmation, and it is one of the easiest places in the paper to lose a mark because the rule has several small parts. The tag is built from two pieces: the auxiliary verb of the statement and a pronoun that stands for the subject. If the statement has no auxiliary, the tag uses do, does or did, and the main verb goes back to its base form.

    The polarity rule is the first thing to apply. A positive statement takes a negative tag, and a negative statement takes a positive tag. You are coming, aren't you? and You aren't coming, are you? are both correct. The tag never repeats the polarity of the statement. A sentence such as She is a doctor, is she? is wrong because both halves are positive; the correct tag is isn't she?

    The tense and the auxiliary must match the statement exactly. If the statement uses is, the tag uses isn't; if it uses has, the tag uses hasn't; if it uses will, the tag uses won't. If the statement uses a simple present with no auxiliary, the tag uses don't or doesn't; if it uses a simple past with no auxiliary, the tag uses didn't. The main verb is not repeated in the tag: She likes tea, doesn't she? and not She likes tea, doesn't like she?

    The pronoun in the tag must be a subject pronoun, not a name and not an object pronoun. Rina is coming, isn't she? is correct; Rina is coming, isn't Rina? is wrong. A noun subject is replaced by he, she, it or they according to number and gender. Two subjects joined by and take they: Rina and Mina are coming, aren't they? A singular subject with each or every also takes they in informal use, though the singular is preferred in careful writing.

    Several special subjects have fixed tags. The subject I am takes aren't I in standard spoken English, not amn't I. The subject let's takes shall we: Let's go, shall we? An imperative takes will you, would you, can you or could you: Close the door, will you? A sentence beginning with nothing, nobody, no one, none or neither takes a positive tag with it or they: Nothing happened, did it? Nobody came, did they?

    Words that make a sentence negative without a visible not also change the tag. Never, hardly, scarcely, barely, seldom, rarely, no longer and little are negative in meaning, so the tag is positive. She never comes late, does she? and He hardly speaks, does he? are correct. A sentence with a negative prefix such as unhappy or impossible is still grammatically positive, so it takes a negative tag: She is unhappy, isn't she?

    The tag is separated by a comma in writing, and the pronoun and auxiliary are contracted in speech and usually in writing. The intonation decides the meaning: a falling tone expects agreement, and a rising tone asks a real question. In the examination only the written form matters, so the safe habit is to check four things in order: the auxiliary, the polarity, the pronoun and the tense.

    Modals: what they do

    A modal is a helping verb that adds meaning such as ability, permission, obligation, advice, possibility or deduction. The main modals are can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will and would, and the semi-modals are ought to, need, dare, used to and had better. A modal is followed by the base form of the main verb, without to, except for ought to, used to and had better. There is no s on a modal: he can, not he cans; she must, not she musts.

    Questions and negatives with modals are made without do. We say Can he swim? and not Does he can swim? and He cannot swim, not He does not can swim. The negative of can is cannot, written as one word, or can't in speech. The negative of must is must not or mustn't for prohibition, while need not or don't have to expresses absence of obligation. The difference between mustn't and needn't is a favourite question: you mustn't smoke means smoking is forbidden, and you needn't smoke means smoking is not necessary.

    Ability is expressed by can in the present and could in the past: She can drive; She could drive when she was eighteen. Permission is expressed by can, may or could, with may the most formal: May I come in? Possibility is expressed by may, might or could: It may rain; It might rain; It could rain. Deduction about the present is expressed by must: The light is on, so he must be at home. Deduction about the past is expressed by must have with the past participle: He must have forgotten.

    Obligation and advice are expressed by must, should, ought to and had better. Must is strong and often comes from the speaker; have to is used for external obligation. Should and ought to give advice and are followed by the base form and to respectively. Had better carries a warning and takes the base form without to: You had better see a doctor. The negative had better not is used for advice against an action.

    The modal perfect is a common exam point. Modal plus have plus past participle looks back at a past action. Should have done means the action was right but was not done, and it carries regret or criticism. Could have done means the action was possible but was not done. Might have done means it was possible. Must have done means the speaker is almost certain. Needn't have done means the action was done but was unnecessary, which is different from needn't do, where the action was not done at all.

    Rules to remember

    • A positive statement takes a negative tag; a negative statement takes a positive tag.
    • The tag repeats the auxiliary and the tense of the statement, and uses a subject pronoun.
    • If there is no auxiliary, the tag uses do, does or did.
    • I am takes aren't I; let's takes shall we; imperatives take will you.
    • Never, hardly, seldom and no longer make the statement negative, so the tag is positive.
    • A modal is followed by the base verb and never takes s or do-support.
    • Mustn't is prohibition; needn't is absence of obligation.
    • Modal plus have plus past participle refers to the past.

    Worked examples

    She has finished her work, hasn't she? The statement uses the auxiliary has, so the tag repeats hasn't, and the subject is replaced by she. The polarity is positive to negative, which is correct. If the statement were She hasn't finished her work, the tag would be has she?

    Nobody phoned, did they? Nobody is negative in meaning, so the tag must be positive. The pronoun for nobody is they in modern usage, and the auxiliary for the past simple is did. The tag is therefore did they?

    You needn't have waited for me. The sentence means that waiting was unnecessary, but it happened. If the person had not waited, the correct sentence would be You needn't wait for me. The difference between needn't have waited and needn't wait is exactly what the modal-perfect question tests.

    He must have missed the train. Must have plus the past participle expresses a strong deduction about the past. If the sentence were about the present, it would be He must be late, without have. The past participle missed is required after have.

    Where marks disappear

    • Repeating the same polarity in both halves.
    • Using a name instead of a pronoun in the tag.
    • Forgetting do-support when the statement has no auxiliary.
    • Using amn't I instead of aren't I.
    • Treating never or hardly as positive and giving a negative tag.
    • Adding s to a modal or using do with a modal.
    • Confusing mustn't with needn't.
    • Using the base verb instead of the past participle after modal plus have.
    Exam tip: For a question tag, check the auxiliary, the polarity, the pronoun and the tense in that order. For a modal, check that the next verb is in the base form and that no do or s has crept in. Both checks take a few seconds and save easy marks.

    Modal: ইহঁতে কি কৰে

    Modal হৈছে সহায়ক ক্ৰিয়া যিয়ে সামৰ্থ্য, অনুমতি, বাধ্যবাধকতা, পৰামৰ্শ, সম্ভাৱনা বা অনুমানৰ অৰ্থ যোগ কৰে। মূল modal হৈছে can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will আৰু would, আৰু semi-modal হৈছে ought to, need, dare, used to আৰু had better। Modal ৰ পিছত মূল ক্ৰিয়াৰ base form আহে, to ৰ বাহিৰে, ought to, used to আৰু had better ৰ ক্ষেত্ৰত। Modal ত s নাই: he can, he cans নহয়; she must, she musts নহয়।

    Modal ৰ প্ৰশ্ন আৰু ঋণাত্মক ৰূপ do ৰ অবিহনে গঠিত হয়। আমি Can he swim? লিখোঁ, Does he can swim? নহয়, আৰু He cannot swim, He does not can swim নহয়। can ৰ ঋণাত্মক cannot, এটা শব্দ হিচাপে, বা কথনত can't। must ৰ ঋণাত্মক must not বা mustn't নিষেধৰ বাবে, আনহাতে need not বা don't have to বাধ্যবাধকতাৰ অনুপস্থিতি বুজায়। mustn't আৰু needn't ৰ পাৰ্থক্য প্ৰিয় প্ৰশ্ন: you mustn't smoke মানে ধূমপান নিষিদ্ধ, you needn't smoke মানে ধূমপান আৱশ্যক নহয়।

    সামৰ্থ্য বৰ্তমানত can আৰু অতীতত could ৰে বুজোৱা হয়: তাই গাড়ী চলাব পাৰে; তাই ওঠৰ বছৰত গাড়ী চলাব পাৰিছিল। অনুমতি can, may বা could ৰে, may আটাইতকৈ আনুষ্ঠানিক: May I come in? সম্ভাৱনা may, might বা could ৰে: বৰষুণ দিব পাৰে। বৰ্তমানৰ অনুমান must ৰে: পোহৰ জ্বলি আছে, গতিকে তেওঁ ঘৰত থাকিবই। অতীতৰ অনুমান must have ৰ লগত past participle ৰে: তেওঁ পাহৰি থাকিবই।

    বাধ্যবাধকতা আৰু পৰামৰ্শ must, should, ought to আৰু had better ৰে বুজোৱা হয়। Must শক্তিশালী আৰু প্ৰায়ে বক্তাৰ পৰা আহে; have to বাহ্যিক বাধ্যবাধকতাৰ বাবে। Should আৰু ought to পৰামৰ্শ দিয়ে আৰু ক্ৰমে base form আৰু to লয়। Had better সতৰ্কবাণী বহন কৰে আৰু to ৰ অবিহনে base form লয়: You had better see a doctor। বিৰোধী কামৰ পৰামৰ্শৰ বাবে had better not ব্যৱহাৰ হয়।

    Modal perfect এটা সাধাৰণ পৰীক্ষা বিন্দু। Modal + have + past participle এ অতীতৰ কামলৈ ঘূৰি চায়। Should have done মানে কামটো কৰা উচিত আছিল কিন্তু কৰা নহ'ল, আৰু ই অনুশোচনা বা সমালোচনা বহন কৰে। Could have done মানে কামটো সম্ভৱ আছিল কিন্তু কৰা নহ'ল। Might have done মানে সম্ভৱ আছিল। Must have done মানে বক্তা প্ৰায় নিশ্চিত। Needn't have done মানে কামটো হৈছে কিন্তু আৱশ্যক নাছিল, যি needn't do ৰ পৰা বেলেগ, য'ত কামটো একেবাৰে হোৱা নাই।

    কিছুমান অৰ্ধ-সহায়ক ক্ৰিয়াৰ টেগত সাৱধানতা লাগে। used to ৰ টেগ didn't: He used to smoke, didn't he? had better ৰ টেগ hadn't: You had better go, hadn't you? would rather ৰ টেগ wouldn't: You would rather stay, wouldn't you? ought to ৰ টেগ shouldn't বা oughtn't: She ought to apologise, shouldn't she? এইবোৰত বাক্যৰ সহায়ক ক্ৰিয়াৰ পৰিৱৰ্তে সেই অৰ্ধ-সহায়ক ক্ৰিয়াৰ সৈতে জড়িত ৰূপটো ব্যৱহাৰ কৰা হয়, আৰু প্ৰশ্নই প্ৰায়ে এই ৰূপবোৰৰ এটা সোধে।

    Modal ৰ তুলনাও প্ৰায়ে সোধা হয়। Can সামৰ্থ্যৰ বাবে, be able to ৰূপ বা কালৰ প্ৰয়োজনত can ৰ ঠাইত: She will be able to come, She will can come নহয়। Must ভিতৰৰ বাধ্যবাধকতাৰ বাবে, have to বাহিৰৰ বাধ্যবাধকতাৰ বাবে, আৰু অতীতত must ৰ সলনি had to: Yesterday I had to work late, Yesterday I must work late নহয়। Would অতীতৰ অভ্যাসৰ বাবে, used to ৰ সৈতে, কিন্তু would কেৱল ক্ৰিয়াৰ বাবে: We would visit our grandmother every summer। This house used to be a school, This house would be a school নহয়, কাৰণ অৱস্থা এটাৰ বাবে used to লাগে।

    মনত ৰখাৰ নিয়ম

    • ধনাত্মক বাক্যই ঋণাত্মক টেগ, ঋণাত্মক বাক্যই ধনাত্মক টেগ লয়।
    • টেগে বাক্যৰ সহায়ক ক্ৰিয়া আৰু কাল পুনৰাবৃত্তি কৰে, আৰু subject pronoun লয়।
    • সহায়ক ক্ৰিয়া নাথাকিলে টেগে do, does বা did লয়।
    • I am ৰ টেগ aren't I; let's ৰ shall we; imperative ৰ will you।
    • never, hardly, seldom, no longer এ বাক্যক ঋণাত্মক কৰে, গতিকে টেগ ধনাত্মক।
    • Modal ৰ পিছত base verb, আৰু modal ত s বা do-support নাই।
    • mustn't নিষেধ; needn't বাধ্যবাধকতাৰ অনুপস্থিতি।
    • Modal + have + past participle এ অতীতলৈ নিৰ্দেশ কৰে।

    সমাধান কৰা উদাহৰণ

    She has finished her work, hasn't she? বাক্যই সহায়ক has লৈছে, গতিকে টেগে hasn't পুনৰাবৃত্তি কৰে, আৰু কৰ্তাক she ৰে সলোৱা হয়। পোলাৰিটি ধনাত্মকৰ পৰা ঋণাত্মক, যি শুদ্ধ। যদি বাক্য She hasn't finished her work হ'লহেঁতেন, টেগ has she? হ'লহেঁতেন।

    Nobody phoned, did they? Nobody অৰ্থত ঋণাত্মক, গতিকে টেগ ধনাত্মক হ'ব লাগে। আধুনিক ব্যৱহাৰত nobody ৰ সৰ্বনাম they, আৰু past simple ৰ সহায়ক did। গতিকে টেগ did they?

    You needn't have waited for me। বাক্যটোৰ অৰ্থ অপেক্ষা আৱশ্যক নাছিল, কিন্তু হৈছে। যদি ব্যক্তিজনে অপেক্ষা নকৰিলেহেঁতেন, শুদ্ধ বাক্য You needn't wait for me হ'লহেঁতেন। needn't have waited আৰু needn't wait ৰ পাৰ্থক্যটোৱেই modal-perfect প্ৰশ্নই পৰীক্ষা কৰে।

    He must have missed the train। Must have ৰ লগত past participle এ অতীতৰ বিষয়ে দৃঢ় অনুমান বুজায়। যদি বাক্যটো বৰ্তমানৰ বিষয়ে হ'লহেঁতেন, তেন্তে He must be late, have ৰ অবিহনে। have ৰ পিছত past participle missed আৱশ্যক।
